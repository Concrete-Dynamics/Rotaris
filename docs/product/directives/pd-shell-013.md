### PD-SHELL-013 — the main window opens clamped to its display's work area, each dimension on its own, and never below its minimum

When a main window opens on a display whose work area is narrower than 1440 logical pixels or lower than 900 logical pixels, the window SHALL open at the lesser of 1440 and the work area's width, and at the lesser of 900 and the work area's height, each dimension taken on its own; the 1.6 aspect of the stated size SHALL NOT be preserved. Neither dimension SHALL fall below the 1000×680 minimum REQ-SHELL-022 states, which governs unchanged: a work area narrower than 1000 or lower than 680 logical pixels opens the window at the minimum in that dimension and the window overflows the work area by the difference. In that overflowing case the window SHALL be placed with its top edge and its left edge inside the work area, so that a height overflow cuts only the bottom of the window — the status strip, REQ-SHELL-024's bottom row, first — and leaves the product-drawn title bar and its three window controls (PD-SHELL-003) wholly on the display; a width overflow, which needs a work area narrower than 1000 logical pixels, cuts the right end of the title bar, its window controls included, and of the status strip, and is the accepted remainder. Outside this case — a work area that holds 1440×900 — the window opens at 1440×900 exactly as REQ-SHELL-022 states. The person may resize freely afterwards, and this directive does not require the main window's size to be remembered. The work area is that of the display the window opens on, for every main window PD-SHELL-001 admits.

**Why, and what it costs.** REQ-SHELL-022 promises that every control a view offers lies wholly inside the window bounds, and PD-SHELL-003 places minimize, maximize/restore and the guarded close at the right end of the product-drawn title bar. A 1920×1080 display at 150 % offers 1280×720 logical pixels ([OQ-508](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/70-spikes-and-later-questions-37.md#oq-508), guide 08 D08-20), so on an ordinary Windows laptop a fixed 1440×900 opens with those three controls off the display and the status strip behind the taskbar: the person's first launch meets a window they cannot maximize, close by pointer or read in full. REQ-SHELL-167 already answers the same problem for the auxiliary windows. Its rule is taken with one departure: the aspect is not preserved, because keeping 1.6 on a 1280-wide work area gives about 1075 px and drops the Workspace view and the status strip below the 1180 px thresholds of REQ-SHELL-047 and REQ-SHELL-059 while 1280 px are available, and the seven primary views are written to flow down to 1000×680 where an auxiliary window's content is fixed. The cost is that the opening size stops being one fixed figure on a small work area, that a work area below the minimum still overflows by the difference (720 less a taskbar is about 672 px, an 8 px remainder cut from the bottom of the status strip), that a work area narrower than 1000 logical pixels cuts the right end of the title bar, the three window controls with it, and that the built-app evidence is owed on a host whose work area is below 1440×900, which `ROTARIS_CAPTURE_SCALE` does not supply. The minimum, the at-minimum checks, the title and the free resize stand; no size is remembered, and a remembered size is a request, not this directive. PD-SHELL-003 extends the same requirement and stands as written; this directive's case is the opening size on a small work area, and the frameless frame governs on every work area. OQ-495 minted nothing: it reads REQ-SHELL-022 at zoom factor 1, the layout inside a window of any size, which is disjoint from the size the window opens at. What reopens it: a measurement that the host already clamps a frameless window to its work area at creation (the directive is then confirmed as built), a macOS host (IC-3), or a request to remember the window's size.
