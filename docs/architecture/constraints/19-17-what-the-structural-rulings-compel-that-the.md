# Architectural constraints extracted from the specification — 17. What the structural rulings compel that the specification does not

Part 19 of 22 of [the constraints](../constraints.md), which indexes every part.

## 17. What the structural rulings compel that the specification does not

Sections 1 to 16 record what the **approved specification** forces. This section records what the
rulings in [`owner-decisions.md`](../owner-decisions.md) force **and the specification does not state**,
which is a different thing and must not be read as the same one. The letter `W` is used because no
section above uses it; it carries no other meaning.

Every row here is labelled **RULING**. A RULING row is binding on the architecture exactly as a HARD row
is, but its authority is an owner decision or an engine behaviour rather than a requirement. Where a row
is derived from a published platform behaviour rather than from a ruling, the derivation says so, because
that half is checkable now and the rest is not.

**This section is much shorter than it was, and the reason is that the amendment pass landed.** SR-1,
SR-2, SR-3 and most of SR-4 are recorded above as HARD, because requirements now carry them: the
one-instance rule (P-14, P-16), the sequence number and gap-driven reconcile (U-01, U-06, U-20, U-21),
the withdrawal of the follower protocol (E-18, E-19), the store's machine-local location and its
observables (D-01, D-13, D-14, D-22, C-04), the export obligation (E-17), the withdrawal of the debounce
(D-09), and SR-7's preparation (B-23). What remains here is what the specification still leaves to the
architecture.

**Second amendment pass, 2026-09-02: this section no longer holds an unmet row.** It carried three
*costs of a ruling that nobody owned* — W-20's cross-machine integration lock, W-22's backup disclosure
and W-23's export list — and all three are now answered by requirement rather than by argument. W-22 and
W-23 are relabelled **MET** and keep their text as the withdrawn claim it is, because both said in terms
that a requirement did not exist and both were read as verified. W-20 keeps its invariant and retires its
escalation. **Two rows are new**: W-24 carries `REQ-SESSION-201`'s backup and restore, which is a HARD
requirement whose *mechanism* is entirely architecture's; W-25 carries the directory-identity rule
`REQ-STARTUP-163` does not state and ADR-0002 had to invent. Rows re-derived or corrected in this pass:
**W-19**, **W-20**, **W-22**, **W-23**, **C-01**, **C-10**, **D-27**, **D-28**, **D-30**.

**Restore-measurement pass, 2026-09-02: three rows are new and all three exist because a mechanism was
run for the first time.** `spikes/backup-restore-mechanism.md` executed `REQ-SESSION-201`'s restore as
ADR-0058 wrote it and it failed in 30 of 30 attempts. **W-29** carries the three rules about replacing a
directory that the failure produced, **W-30** carries the refusal-while-live as a durative condition and
the two obligations it puts on ADR-0002 that ADR-0002 does not state, and **W-31** carries
`REQ-SESSION-202`'s store-establishment rule. **W-24** is re-derived and one of its sentences is
withdrawn in place. *The pattern is worth more than any of the three and is stated once, here and in the
gate verdict: **three mechanisms in this record have now been built for the first time — ADR-0054's
commit step, ADR-0054's completeness check, and this restore — and every one held a defect that no
reading had caught.** It is recorded as ADR-0058 consequence 17. It licenses no universal measurement and
it settles two narrower things: an unrun mechanism is not evidence that it works, and a defect found this
way belongs in the record in place rather than being repaired quietly in code.*

| W-05 | **Every write transaction begins `IMMEDIATE` and is short.** Two writer processes contend up front rather than deadlocking on a lock upgrade.
