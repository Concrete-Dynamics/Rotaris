# MCP Server Integration — The Inbound Surface

Part 7 of 7 of [domain 16](../16-mcp-integration.md), which indexes every part. Defines REQ-MCP-119 – REQ-MCP-130.

### The Inbound Surface

Every requirement above this section is the product as an MCP **client**. This section is the other direction — the one surface the product opens for external agents. **Its identifiers are decided, not derived:** `REQ-MCP-119` to `REQ-MCP-130` carry the decision of [ADR-0067](../../architecture/adr/0067-the-loopback-mcp-server-for-external-agents.md), taken under the owner's ruling [R-094](../../product/requests.md#r-094), and they are no evidence about the behavioural target. The exclusion they narrow is `REQ-EVENTS-178`, and `REQ-STARTUP-002` and `REQ-STARTUP-160` with it; all four dispositions are [`PD-EVENTS-001`](../../product/directives/pd-events-001.md#pd-events-001)'s.

- **REQ-MCP-119** — The product SHALL offer exactly one inbound surface: an MCP server serving **the running instance** over the streamable-HTTP transport, bound to the loopback interface alone — `127.0.0.1` and, where the platform supplies it, `::1` — on an **ephemeral port** the operating system chooses. It SHALL NEVER bind a fixed port, `0.0.0.0`, or any address another machine can reach; "loopback" is the product's existing notion, reused here and not defined a second time (REQ-TOOLS-087, REQ-SANDBOX-018). No `stdio` transport SHALL be offered — a foreign agent that spawned the application would meet its hand-over rather than the running instance (REQ-STARTUP-161), and the streams of a child belong to the client that started it (REQ-MCP-113) — and no server-sent-events transport SHALL be offered either. The listener SHALL belong to the running instance and SHALL start no second one.

- **REQ-MCP-120** — The surface SHALL be off in every build until the user turns it on, and turning it on SHALL last for that session alone: nothing SHALL enable it by itself, quitting the application SHALL revoke it, and the choice SHALL NOT persist across launches. Its control SHALL sit in the register of the per-server enable toggle of the MCP settings category (REQ-MCP-091), which SHALL show the port and the token while it is on. **The surface SHALL add no configuration key:** it is not an `mcp_servers` entry, it is not authored in a `mcp.json` discovery file or in the layered YAML configuration (REQ-MCP-025), and it belongs to no persona's carried servers (REQ-MCP-038). Its state is therefore never the configured `enabled` value of REQ-CONFIG-465 and never a session override of REQ-MCP-094; it is a third thing, and the category SHALL say so rather than let a user take it for a toggle that did not stick.

- **REQ-MCP-121** — A bearer token SHALL be generated when the exposure is switched on, shown to the user once, required on every request to the surface, and written neither to the application log nor into any session record. Same-UID reasoning SHALL NOT be treated as the boundary: every process of the user can see a loopback port, so the token is what separates a caller from a bystander. Revoking the exposure SHALL invalidate the token with it, and a request carrying no token or a stale one SHALL be refused without reaching the run host.

- **REQ-MCP-122** — The `Origin` and `Host` of every request SHALL be checked against loopback, and a mismatch SHALL be refused before the request is served. This closes DNS rebinding from the user's own browser — the hazard a long-lived localhost HTTP tool surface carries and the one-callback sign-in listener (REQ-PROVIDER-029 to REQ-PROVIDER-031) does not.

- **REQ-MCP-123** — The surface SHALL expose exactly two MCP tools, and no third. `rotaris_start_run` SHALL start a run in an **already open** workspace and return that run's handle (REQ-SESSION-080); `rotaris_run_status` SHALL read that run's state and stop reason in the product's own status vocabulary. The surface SHALL NOT proxy a tool call, SHALL NOT expose a tool the product does not otherwise have, and SHALL NOT let a caller invoke anything a run of the user's own could not: an external agent drives **the run**, and the run does its own work through the ordinary gate.

- **REQ-MCP-124** — A run a caller starts through the surface SHALL be an ordinary run of the one lifecycle (REQ-SESSION-080) and SHALL have **no** permission, approval or sandbox model of its own: the gate, the approval chain, the sandbox and the workspace fence that govern a run the user starts SHALL govern it unchanged. It SHALL leave durable evidence exactly as any other run does, so its session's history is the requirement of `REQ-EVENTS-081` — which spans every run through the shared lifecycle and expressly does not close its enumeration of the ways one can be started. The surface SHALL NOT be a second run engine, a second store or a second session writer: one writer per session holds (REQ-EVENTS-098), and the session lock that right is (REQ-SESSION-083) is held by the process that owns it and never by the listener.

- **REQ-MCP-125** — Every approval and every question a run started this way raises SHALL be answered **by a person, at the desktop**, and never by the calling agent. The surface SHALL NOT forward an approval or a question to the caller, SHALL NOT accept an answer to one from the caller, and SHALL NOT offer an approval resolver of its own; a caller MAY observe that its run is waiting for one and MAY NOT answer one. The wait SHALL be the ordinary wait — REQ-CONFIG-126's `approval_timeout_seconds`, whose `0` means wait without a limit and whose expiry resolves as a denial, and REQ-TOOLS-177's teardown, which cancels the waiting approvals when the approval host is discarded — and SHALL NOT be shortened, skipped, or resolved as approved because a caller is waiting on it.

- **REQ-MCP-126** — At most one externally started run SHALL be active at a time. A request that arrives while one is active SHALL be **refused with a stated reason**, and SHALL NEVER be queued, deferred or run later on the first run's behalf. A per-run ceiling on this path SHALL exist in principle; its numbers are *reserved*, naming a decision the owner has not taken.

- **REQ-MCP-127** — The listener SHALL live in a `utilityProcess` of its own, forked from the one shipped binary as every other process of the instance is (ADR-0002): **not** in main, which stays the instance's front door on its 100 ms interface budget (REQ-SHELL-150), and **not** in the run host, which keeps every session lock and the sole right to write a session's history (REQ-EVENTS-098) and gains no inbound network face. The listener SHALL speak to the run host in the run host's existing terms and SHALL NOT write a session's store. This is the amendment to ADR-0002's "four process kinds, and no others" that this surface owes, and it is stated here rather than left open.

- **REQ-MCP-128** — What a caller asked for SHALL be recorded where the product records its own acts: the run's record, the session's history and the session's timeline SHALL carry it as they carry a run the user started (REQ-EVENTS-081). The surface SHALL mint **no store and no event type** — the catalog of REQ-EVENTS-041 keeps its types, and what the surface records is written in the vocabulary the product already writes.

- **REQ-MCP-129** — The surface SHALL NOT reach beyond this machine and SHALL NOT acquire a publication dimension: no port-forwarding, tunnel or funnel SHALL be opened for it, no second or headless executable SHALL be published or installed for it (REQ-STARTUP-002), and it SHALL carry no multi-user, account or role dimension — the one user at the desk is whom it serves.

- **REQ-MCP-130** — The surface SHALL have no capability beyond starting and reading runs: nothing reachable through it SHALL read a file, run a command, touch Git, reach the network, mint or return a credential, read a secret, or manage MCP servers. It SHALL leave the MCP **client** path of this domain unchanged — the configuration model (REQ-MCP-025), the per-persona grants (REQ-MCP-065) and a run's own server connections are exactly what they were — and it SHALL NOT be a `mcp_servers` entry, carry a persona's tools, or appear in a prompt (REQ-MCP-101).
