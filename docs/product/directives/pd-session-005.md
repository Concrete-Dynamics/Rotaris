### PD-SESSION-005 — an unreadable restore target's lock records are reported as unreadable, not counted

Where REQ-SESSION-201's restore is admitted over a target store that failed C1's integrity check (`workspace.store-corrupt` held, the assessment's `target.unreadable`), the restore SHALL NOT open or read the target to count lock records naming another machine, and its confirmation SHALL NOT state how many there are. In place of the count, the single first question SHALL state that the store failed its check, quoting SQLite's report; that whether it holds lock records naming another machine, and how many, cannot be read; what such records would mean, that nothing on this machine writes under them because no store was handed to the run host under the held refusal; and the clause's risk unchanged, that if the store is reachable from another machine a run there may be writing to it now and the restore replaces what it writes. Every other word of the foreign-lock clause SHALL govern as written: one condition and never one per session, no clearing one at a time as a precondition, no plain refusal, the risk stated and not only the condition, and proceeding never automatic, never the default and never pre-selected, Cancel the default of that question. The workspace service SHALL displace an unreadable target only under a decision that records that confirmation (`targetUnreadable` in `performRestore`'s decisions), which `restoreFlow` SHALL send only after the first question was answered Restore, and no second confirmation SHALL be asked for the same condition. A readable target is untouched: its lock records are counted and reported together in their own confirmation as the clause states. This directive SHALL be reopened by salvage (ADR-0058 part 11, steps 2 and 3) being built, since a salvaged copy could yield a count that is evidence, or by an owner's request that the damaged target be read for one.

**Why, and what it costs.** The clause's condition is the store holding such records, not the product knowing that it does, and a damaged store can hold them: the corrupt fixture in `packages/workspace/src/workspace.test.ts` holds one on pages the restore never reads. OQ-477's invariant that nothing opens the damaged target except C1's read-only `checkIntegrity` is what keeps `corruptRefusal`'s promise and leaves the kept directory unchanged as salvage's input, and a number read from damaged pages is not evidence (REQ-SESSION-089's reasoning): "0 lock records" read off them would be false reassurance at the one moment the user decides whether to overwrite a store another machine may be writing to. The cost is that the user gets no count and cannot tell a damaged store with no foreign locks from one with several; what they get is the risk, said as an unknown, inside the first question rather than in a warning of its own; and the record gains a directive for a rare case and a correction to a same-day ruling, which is cheaper than a record whose own fixture contradicts its claim of compliance.
