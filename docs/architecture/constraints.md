# Architectural constraints extracted from the specification

This is the evidence base for every decision in [`adr/`](adr/). Each entry states what the approved
behavioural specification **forces**, and cites the requirement identifiers that force it. Nothing here
is a design choice; where a design choice was made, it is in an ADR and it cites an entry from this
document.

Entries are labelled:

- **HARD** — the specification fixes an observable that only some designs can produce.
- **HINT** — not fixed, but one design makes several requirements natural and the alternatives awkward.
- **FREE** — genuinely an implementation choice, recorded so it is not later mistaken for a constraint.
- **CONFLICT** — two live requirements cannot both be satisfied as written; carried to
  [`open-questions.md`](open-questions.md).

Domains 17 and 18 are retired; no `REQ-TUI` or `REQ-CLI` identifier appears anywhere below. Every
capability those documents carried is cited through the desktop requirement that owns it.

## The second half of the evidence base: `docs/spec/resolutions/`

**A requirement is not the whole of what the specification says about it.** Alongside the twenty-one
domain documents sits [`docs/spec/resolutions/`](../spec/resolutions/) — twenty-one records, one per
domain, disposing of every open question that domain's draft raised, with the disposition, the decision
taken, and the requirement identifiers it touched. An architect who reads only `docs/spec/` reads half
the evidence.

They matter here for three reasons, and each has already changed an entry below.

- **A resolution sometimes narrows what a requirement licenses.** `REQ-UIBENCH-050` says both structural
  counts are "defined against the interface framework the product is built on", which reads as a licence
  for the product to say which of its own elements are visual. Row 29 of
  `resolutions/21-ui-performance-benchmark.md` fixes the counting rules "in framework-neutral terms" with
  an observable test, and row 13 renames a field because it "leaks one GUI toolkit's vocabulary into a
  schema". That reading is what makes I-10 disputed and OQ-28 blocking.
- **A resolution sometimes widens it, or records why a figure is what it is.** Row 23 of
  `resolutions/02-run-monitoring-steering.md` records why `REQ-MONITOR-223`'s comparison point is 10,000
  rows; row 1 of `resolutions/07-sessions-persistence.md` records that `REQ-SESSION-071`'s write-volume
  bound "is the whole contract and no further coalescing is required".
- **A resolution is sometimes the only place a withdrawal is recorded.** `resolutions/11` records that
  the startup domain's self-invocation flag was withdrawn in favour of the `REQ-MCP-033` sentinel, and
  `resolutions/21` question 11 records that the requirement which once defined an argument form for the
  benchmark's iteration child is withdrawn — "a third party running one iteration directly is not a
  capability the product offers". Both are why P-13 below now says the specification fixes **one**
  reserved token rather than two.

The rule for this document: where a resolution record narrows, widens or withdraws what a requirement
appears to license, the entry cites the resolution alongside the requirement. A resolution never
overrides a requirement's text — it is evidence of what that text was taken to mean when it was
approved.

## The nine structural rulings, and what the specification now compels

[`owner-decisions.md`](owner-decisions.md) carries nine structural rulings — SR-1 (one running
application instance), SR-2 (a run this application does not own cannot be viewed), SR-3 (the polling
cadences are withdrawn), SR-4 (a store, not files, for what only the application reads), SR-5 (a
workspace held by another machine is refused, and the refusal is a safety feature), SR-6 (the export
obligation covers every record a user could previously read), SR-7 (the `onefile` portable prepares
itself once), SR-8 (`REQ-ORCH-155`'s 100 ms is a percentile), SR-9 (two gaps SR-4 and SR-6 left) — plus
rulings on ESC-1 to ESC-18, of which ESC-15 (the store is machine-local) and ESC-16 (a store that will
not open means no run) reach further into this document than any other — **and, since 2026-09-01, rulings
on ESC-19, ESC-20 and ESC-21**, which the owner asked to be decided by comparison with seven other coding
harnesses rather than from first principles. Those three reach this document through W-20 (the
integration lock now refuses on replicated storage), W-22 and W-23 (both relabelled **MET**), W-24 (the
whole-store backup and restore) and D-30 (the index `REQ-TRACE-089` permits).

**The amendment pass those rulings authorised has landed, and the rows below have been re-derived
against the amended text rather than patched.** That distinction matters: a row that recorded a
*mechanism* the specification fixed is not "differently met" now, it is **wrong at the source**, because
the specification withdrew the mechanism and states an observable in its place. Every such row was
re-read against the requirement as it now stands.

**What the amendment actually did, in one paragraph, because it is not what a reader expects.** It did
**not** write SR-4's vocabulary into the specification. No live requirement anywhere names a database, a
table, an index, a query or a schema migration (**D-22**). What it did was replace fixed paths, fixed
byte formats and fixed write disciplines with **observables over a store whose form it does not fix**:
one workspace plus one session id resolves one record (`REQ-SESSION-001`); the store is machine-local
and found by a workspace identifier (`REQ-SESSION-197`, `REQ-SESSION-198`); a save is all-or-nothing
(`REQ-SESSION-028`); a reader may not delay the writer (`REQ-EVENTS-098`); a listing's cost grows with
the number of sessions and with nothing else (`REQ-SESSION-042`); the line-delimited form is the
**export's** format and not the store's (`REQ-EVENTS-090`, `REQ-EVENTS-166`). Engine, schema and indexes
are therefore architecture's to choose — and architecture's to be wrong about.

separate process using a different binding read a terminal status back immediately at the weaker setting

**section 17**, labelled RULING rather than HARD, so a reader can tell the two apart at a glance. That
section is much shorter than it was: most of what it held has landed above. What remains there is what
the specification still does not say.

## 17. What the structural rulings compel that the specification does not

Sections 1 to 16 record what the **approved specification** forces. This section records what the
rulings in [`owner-decisions.md`](owner-decisions.md) force **and the specification does not state**,
which is a different thing and must not be read as the same one. The letter `W` is used because no
section above uses it; it carries no other meaning.

Every row here is labelled **RULING**. A RULING row is binding on the architecture exactly as a HARD row
is, but its authority is an owner decision or an engine behaviour rather than a requirement. Where a row
is derived from a published platform behaviour rather than from a ruling, the derivation says so, because
that half is checkable now and the rest is not.

**This section is much shorter than it was, and the reason is that the amendment pass landed.** SR-1,
SR-2, SR-3 and most of SR-4 are recorded above as HARD, because requirements now carry them: the

   resolved **by subject**: the transcript was never followed by timed state reads, while non-transcript
   state for a *foreign* session legitimately was, on a 750 ms poll. Both halves of that reconciliation
   are gone. SR-2 removed the foreign session and SR-3 removed both cadences, so `REQ-SESSION-071`'s rule
   now has no exception and nothing needs reconciling. Retained as a tombstone because the resolution was
   cited by component, and a reader who finds it cited should see that it was dissolved rather than
   forgotten. What replaces it is not a second reading but a mechanism: U-01's sequence number and U-06's
   gap-driven reconcile.
3. **H-12.** The verification-skip path signals the process group; the hook path must not. Both are
   deliberate; they are two execution paths and **must not share a kill implementation**.
4. **P-02 versus P-14.** "A backgrounded run continues in the same process" versus a separate worker
   process being listed among the venues: `REQ-MONITOR-299` **permits** a topology while
   `REQ-SESSION-190` **fixes** the observable. Detaching changes which views follow the run; it does not
   migrate it.
5. **REQ-ORCH-039 versus REQ-ORCH-116.** A delegation to the acceptance role is refused only on a
   `failed` blocking check, while the completion gate is also left unsatisfied by `timeout` and
   `skipped`. **The specification states the asymmetry is deliberate and must be preserved**; recorded so
   it is not "fixed".
6. **M-17.** Unknown fields are accepted on consumed remote payloads and rejected in the persisted
   snapshot. Deliberate, and in opposite directions; the two readers must not share a schema mode.
7. **D-16 versus D-09 — DISSOLVED, not resolved, and the requirement says so itself.** This
   reconciliation recorded a contradiction between "every change is persisted before it is published to
   any view" and "saves for `running`/`idle` are debounced at 10.0 s". **Both premises are gone.**
   `REQ-SESSION-067` withdraws the window outright and gives the same reason this reconciliation reached
   from the other side — the window "was the interval in which a change could be published before it was
   recorded, which persist-before-publish (`REQ-MONITOR-221`) forbids and which now cannot arise" — and
   `REQ-MONITOR-221` now states its own reconciliation with `REQ-MONITOR-218`: it is satisfiable
   alongside it "because persistence carries no delay of its own". There is nothing left to reconcile.

   *A previous pass wrote here that the debounce "narrows to the session record", where
   "`REQ-SESSION-071`'s stated observable of at most twelve writes per minute still holds". **That is now
   wrong on both halves and must not be cited.*** `REQ-SESSION-067` forbids deferring, parking and
   coalescing outright, so there is no narrowed window; and `REQ-SESSION-071` states that "no bound on
   write volume is stated and none is needed", so the observable the narrowing was kept to preserve does
   not exist. Every requested save is durable when it is requested, and two saves requested microseconds
   apart produce two commits.

   *What the dissolution cost, recorded because nothing else records it.* The window bounded exactly one
   thing that is still a file: the human-readable summary. `REQ-SESSION-028` rebuilds it on **every**
   save and calls it "the one part written as a file"; D-06's ladder spends up to about 1.55 s of backoff
   on it; and it is the one write in the product where a foreign handle — anti-malware, or the user's own
   editor reached through `REQ-SESSION-191` — can make a save-caused write take a second. Every other
   document the window bounded became a record; this one did not. It is derived and off the critical path
   (`REQ-SESSION-006`: "it is on no path a run waits for"), so nothing a loader reads is at risk — but
   `REQ-SESSION-028`'s "rebuilt by the same save" also admits a blocking reading, and no requirement
   settles which. ADR-0008 takes the non-blocking one and states the point of wording explicitly.

   *Unchanged, and the part most easily lost:* intra-row growth under U-03's 50 ms tick sits **outside**
   the persist-before-publish rule, because `REQ-EVENTS-052` gives an incrementally produced row two
   events "and none for any state in between", and `REQ-SESSION-196` says why that is conformant rather
   than a gap — partially streamed text is not restored, "because [it] describe[s] a moment rather than
   the session". Commits being cheap is **not** a reason to start committing per tick; per-tick commits
   are forbidden for the same reason `REQ-MONITOR-219` forbids per-token publication. A conformance test
   that diffs a live surface against the session record mid-run fails correctly behaving code.
