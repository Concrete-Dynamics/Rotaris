### PD-MONITOR-009 — the Overview is the one monitoring view: it holds the delegation graph, its run controls and the agent activity table, and the two delegation settings leave monitoring

1. **Where the graph and the table stand.** The Overview MUST present the live delegation graph and the agent activity table that REQ-MONITOR-274 gives a mission view, with the same actions under the same terms: open an agent, pause the selection (graceful, valid for the run-level selection alone, carrying its availability explanation) and cancel the selection (marked destructive, confirmed). Pause and cancel sit under the graph. No other primary view presents them, and no mission view exists ([`PD-SHELL-015`](pd-shell-015.md#pd-shell-015)).
2. **The graph card.** The graph is a card of the Overview's right column and is that column's **first** card, above the cloud-credit tile; the subscription-limits card and the improvement-proposals card follow in REQ-MONITOR-173's order. It takes the place of that requirement's agent-tree card and carries no link to another view. It holds a selectable row for the run above the tree, the tree, and under them Pause, "Cancel selection", with an agent selected the action that opens it, and Pause's explanation.
3. **The table card.** The table is a card of the Overview's left column, under the metric cards and above the sessions card; it is the place REQ-MONITOR-116 gives the Overview for the agents of the run, and it lists every agent of the focused session, as REQ-MONITOR-274's table does. It refreshes row by row, and, the Overview being mounted only while shown, refreshes nothing while hidden and catches up when shown (REQ-MONITOR-039, REQ-MONITOR-275's second clause). Where the card is too narrow for one line a row, a row MUST give way to more lines and MUST keep every value, each figure still named by its column's words; no value is dropped, the card MUST NOT scroll sideways, and no control of a row is reached by horizontal scrolling (REQ-SHELL-022). The Overview keeps REQ-MONITOR-165's two columns at every width.
4. **Selection.** The graph's and the table's selection is the one shared selection of REQ-MONITOR-042: a row chosen in either is the row marked in both, in the Workspace sidebar and in the inspector.
5. **Refusal.** A pause requested there for a selection that is not the run is refused as REQ-MONITOR-276 states, with the explanation shown and no control sent.
6. **The two delegation settings.** The strategy selector and the depth control of REQ-MONITOR-275's first clause MUST NOT be drawn on the Overview or on any other monitoring surface. Their one editor is the Settings view's Runtime category ("Delegation strategy", "Depth cap"; REQ-CONFIG-321, REQ-CONFIG-112), on that form's terms. The graph card MUST show the configured strategy as text, said to apply to the next run, and MUST carry one control that opens that category (an action REQ-SHELL-115 already permits); the depth cap stays where REQ-MONITOR-169 shows it, in the agents card's caption.
7. **Where a requirement or a directive names the mission view.** The tree surfaces of REQ-MONITOR-038 are two, the Workspace sidebar and the Overview, held to that requirement's parity and budget. "The mission view" in REQ-MONITOR-130 and "mission graph" in REQ-MONITOR-213 are the Overview's graph card. REQ-TRACE-475's link from an execution unit to "the mission view for its agents" leads to the Overview, carrying the session identifier as that requirement states. A directive that names the Mission view or its table ([`PD-ORCH-005`](pd-orch-005.md#pd-orch-005) clause 13, [`PD-MONITOR-003`](pd-monitor-003.md#pd-monitor-003), [`PD-CONFIG-003`](pd-config-003.md#pd-config-003)'s "Mission-table row") names these two cards; none of them changes otherwise.

**Why, and what it costs.** Decision D2 of [the information-architecture map](../../design/information-architecture.md). On the built application at `880a08a9` the Mission view (`pnpm mission-proof`, launch `painted`, 1440×900) held three cards: the delegation graph, 340 px wide; the activity table, about 890 px; and "Delegation settings". The Overview during a run (`pnpm overview-proof`, launch `run`) already drew the same tree in a read-only card of its 340 px right column, under a link reading "Open Mission", and left the lower half of its 912 px left column empty; and REQ-MONITOR-116 had always given the Overview a card listing the agents of the run, which was never built there. So the agent set stood in three views, the figures of a session and the agents producing them were one navigation apart, and the graph card and the table fit the Overview's two columns at the widths they already had (after building: 340 px and 906 px at 1440×900). The two settings were a second editor for keys the Runtime category edits, applying to the next run while standing beside the controls of the current one, and writing at once where that category's form waits for Save: two commit behaviours for one key.

What a user can now do that they could not: read a session's tokens, tool calls and repository state beside its agents, and pause or cancel from the view that shows what the run has cost; select an agent in the Overview's tree and have that selection hold in the Workspace (the Overview's old card kept a selection of its own). What it costs:

- **The minimum window.** Below 1180 px the Mission view stacked its cards and gave the table the whole width. The Overview keeps its two columns there, which leaves the table's card about 460 px at 1000×680, so a row takes three lines (clause 3; measured after building: the card's scroll width equals its client width, 458 px, and every row control lies inside it).
- **The order of the right column.** The cloud-credit tile, whose leading place REQ-MONITOR-173 argues from quota reachability, now stands second: a card that carries the run's Pause and Cancel must be on screen at REQ-SHELL-022's minimum height (measured after building at 1000×680: both buttons end at y = 393 of 680), and the credit tile is read-only.
- **The keyboard path.** On Mission the run row was the first stop after the rail; on the Overview it follows the header's two controls, the table's two controls a row (up to forty with twenty agents), the session links and the column divider. Ctrl+2 and the Workspace's own agent header and inspector remain the short path to steering.
- **The sessions card** stands under the table, so with many agents it starts lower than it did; it is the surface OQ-506 ruled the returning operator reads, and with no agents the table card is its empty statement and costs it about 100 px.
- **The agent set is still drawn twice on one view**, as a tree and as a table, as it was on Mission; the agents card's counts make a third reading of it. The Overview's audit of 2026-11-02 already called the tree and the count "one stat twice".
- **A click on the summary view can open a window.** With `interface/agentPopout` on, choosing an agent in the graph opens its persona window, as it did on Mission.
- **The strategy** is shown and no longer edited on a monitoring surface: changing it or the depth takes Settings, Runtime, the field and Save, where it was one select on Mission that wrote at once.

**Removed, with its argument.** The strategy selector and the depth control as controls of a monitoring view (clause 6): a setting for the next run does not belong among the controls of this one, and one key keeps one commit behaviour; the capability stays whole in Settings › Runtime, and the strategy stays readable where it was. Not taken: folding Mission into the Workspace (three panes and a compact mode already; the table is read when not typing); moving only the table to the Overview and leaving steering to the Workspace alone, which the critique proposed (an operator who sees a run burn tokens on the view that shows the cost should be able to stop it there, as on Mission); keeping the name "Mission" for the merged view; stacking the Overview to one column below 1180 px (REQ-MONITOR-165's two columns would have to give way, and the graph with its controls would fall below the sessions list).

> **Narrows:** REQ-MONITOR-274, REQ-MONITOR-275, REQ-MONITOR-276, REQ-MONITOR-173, REQ-MONITOR-038, REQ-MONITOR-130, REQ-MONITOR-213, REQ-TRACE-475

Its case on each is where the surface stands; on REQ-MONITOR-275, also that its first clause's two controls are not drawn; on REQ-MONITOR-173, also the order of the right column's first card. Every other clause of each stands as written, and REQ-MONITOR-042, REQ-MONITOR-116, REQ-MONITOR-165, REQ-MONITOR-169 and REQ-MONITOR-174 govern in full. It is disjoint from [`PD-ORCH-005`](pd-orch-005.md#pd-orch-005)'s cases on REQ-MONITOR-130 and REQ-MONITOR-213 (what a paused agent and a resting run present), and it leaves [`PD-LIBRARY-002`](pd-library-002.md#pd-library-002)'s extension of REQ-MONITOR-173 (the retrospective gesture on the proposals card) as it is.
