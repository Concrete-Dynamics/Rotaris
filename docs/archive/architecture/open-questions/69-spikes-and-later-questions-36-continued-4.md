**OQ-477 — How is a store refused as corrupt resolved?** *(REQ-SESSION-089, REQ-SESSION-201, REQ-SESSION-202, ADR-0058's recovery ladder, OQ-281. Raised 2026-11-01 by hardening C1, which built the refusal.)*

**What is open.** The workspace service's open now runs `PRAGMA quick_check` before it migrates, and a store that fails it is refused as `workspace.store-corrupt`, naming the store and SQLite's report, held by main beside the other store refusals (`main/store-notice.ts`). Nothing resolves it. ADR-0058's ladder (salvage into a fresh store, the exports of what it recovered, moving the damaged store aside and starting an empty one, and naming the restore as a remedy the user may take) is unbuilt. REQ-SESSION-201's restore is refused for a window with no workspace identifier unless the held refusal is `workspace.store-missing` (`main/store-resolution.ts`, `placeOf`), and Settings › Project disables it the same way. Admitting it here leaves open what the assessment says about a target it cannot read: its sessions and its lock rows may sit on the damaged pages, and REQ-SESSION-201's first dialog states how many sessions the replaced store holds. It also leaves open whether the inspection may open the target read-write, whose close checkpoints the log the refusal kept. Whether the notice should carry an action, and which, follows from that.

**What the build proceeds under.** The refusal is held and raised as an error notice with no action. Its detail says the store was left as it was found, its write-ahead log included, and that no session starts until it is resolved. It names no remedy the product cannot perform. The person resolves it by hand. A copy may be taken and repaired outside the product; and once the store is moved out of its place by hand, the place reads as REQ-SESSION-202's empty place, where Settings › Project admits the restore and the new store (OQ-281).

**What decides it.** A ruling on which of the ladder's steps, and whether the restore, are offered while the refusal is held; what the restore's assessment reports for a target that fails the check; and the notice's action.

**Ruled 2026-11-04 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended that Settings › Project › Session store resolve a held `workspace.store-corrupt` as OQ-281 resolves `workspace.store-missing`, admitting REQ-SESSION-201's restore and a deliberate "Set the damaged store aside and start a new one…" (ADR-0058's ladder step 4), with a read-only, log-keeping read of the target tried for the assessment and the session count stated as unreadable where it cannot be trusted. Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended the same place and the same two actions under one stricter invariant, that nothing in either flow opens the damaged target at all, the assessment reporting it as present, failed its check and unreadable, with a perform-time re-check through C1's own `checkIntegrity`. Ruled: B's, with no directive. While `workspace.store-corrupt` is held for a window's workspace, Settings › Project › Session store is where it is resolved (OQ-281's place, REQ-SHELL-023's Project category), and the error notice carries OQ-281's `open-settings`/`project` action, withdrawn once an open succeeds; two actions are offered there and only while the refusal is held: REQ-SESSION-201's restore, and a deliberate "Set the damaged store aside and start a new one…", ADR-0058 part 11's step 4, Cancel the default and the cancel on both. Both move the store's directory aside whole, `workspace.db` with its `-wal` and `-shm` byte for byte, under the restore's own `displaced` stamp (`claimAsideName`), keep it, and name the kept place on success; neither deletes or repairs anything in place (ADR-0058 part 11, "never deletes a store and never repairs one in place"). Step 5 is met by the notice's detail and Settings naming the restore as a remedy the user may take, without looking for or offering an artifact. Steps 2 and 3, salvage and its exports, are not built under this ruling and stay owed: the directory set aside is kept unchanged as their input, so deferring them loses nothing, and the ladder's "salvage first" ordering is reported back into part 11 beside its reason, kept and not repaired, with what salvage needs of the engine raised as its own question when it is taken up. The governing invariant is that no flow opens the damaged target except through the one open the record has measured to leave it as found, C1's `checkIntegrity` with its read-only holder (ADR-0058 part 11, first execution 2026-11-01): the assessment's `openStoreForInspection` would open a target holding a `-wal` and no `-shm` read-write, `restoreStore`'s `openStore(dbFile, { mustExist: true })` always does, and either close checkpoints the log into the damaged file and deletes it, which breaks the promise `corruptRefusal` already makes. So the assessment reports a target that fails the check as present, failed, contents unreadable, with no session count and no lock list, each stated as unknown, because a number read from damaged pages is not evidence (REQ-SESSION-089's own reasoning). The restore's first question says the store here failed its integrity check, quotes SQLite's report, says how many sessions it holds cannot be read, that it is moved aside and kept unchanged while the backup's sessions take its place, and that every session in it will no longer be listed; the establishing action says the same of what it sets aside, that it recovers nothing, and that this machine holds no other copy. That meets REQ-SESSION-201 as written: "what the restore will do to the store that is there now" is conveyed, what becomes of the target's sessions is an outcome the user chose having been told it, and no session survives or vanishes quietly, the requirement asking for the consequence and not a count. Its live-session refusal holds by construction: under a held refusal no store was handed to the run host (REQ-SESSION-089, REQ-SESSION-198, OQ-281's `!missing` reasoning), so `runs.active` is skipped and `judge()` sees no rows. Its foreign-lock clause binds "where the target store holds such records", which the product cannot establish without reading them, so the first question states instead that any lock records could not be read and the risk the clause requires, that a run on another machine reaching this store may be writing now and the restore replaces what it writes, with proceeding not pre-selected; that is the clause's purpose met on a target it must not read, and no words are departed from. REQ-SESSION-202's terms carry over to the establishing action: deliberate, never a default, never a side effect of an open or a run, told what was found and that it recovers nothing. At perform, both actions re-run C1's check on the store then in the place and refuse if it now passes ("the store in its place now passes its check; open the workspace again"), the analogue of `checkEmptyPlace`'s `store-present`, so a store repaired by hand in the meantime is never set aside on a stale refusal; a perform that fails leaves the store exactly as it was, by the restore's own reversal. What executes: `placeOf` (`main/store-resolution.ts`) returns which refusal is held, `missing` or `corrupt`, and `restoreFlow` and `establishFlow` admit both with a third wording; `storeRefusalNotice` (`main/store-notice.ts`) attaches `OPEN_PROJECT_SETTINGS` to `workspace.store-corrupt`, and `corruptRefusal` (`workspace-service/store-open.ts`) names the two remedies and the by-hand route; `assessRestore` (`packages/workspace/src/restore.ts`) gains a `target.unreadable { report }` branch that opens nothing; `restoreStore` (`packages/store/src/restore.ts`) gains a `targetUnreadable` option under which no service handle is opened and step 8 displaces the directory whole; `establish.ts` generalises `establishInEmptyPlace` to a still-failing store re-checked through `checkIntegrity`; `SettingsView.tsx` offers both; pinned by `store-resolution.test.ts`, `store-establish.test.ts` and the restore tests over a corrupt fixture with a live `-wal` and no `-shm`, asserting that the displaced directory holds `workspace.db` and `-wal` byte for byte and that nothing opened the target, and by a corrupt-store case in `pnpm store-resolution-proof`; the report-back goes into ADR-0058 part 11 beside C1's paragraph, with one row in `first-executions.md` and W-31's note. Reopened by salvage being taken up (its own question on the engine's recovery route), by a measured `quick_check` false positive, which the kept directory bounds to a store unlisted and not lost, or by an owner's request that the damaged target be read for a count after all.
