# Desktop Shell, Navigation, and Window Management — Shell accessibility contract

Part 5 of 7 of [domain 12](../12-desktop-shell-windows.md), which indexes every part. Defines REQ-SHELL-144 – REQ-SHELL-168.

### Shell accessibility contract

- **REQ-SHELL-144**: Two contrast thresholds apply: 4.5:1 for normal text, and 3:1 for large text — 18 px or larger, or 14 px or larger when bold — and for non-text boundaries such as borders, focus rings and icons. Measured over all seven primary views and every tab page inside them: no interactive control announces an empty accessible name, and every rendered label meets its threshold against the background actually painted behind it once all styling has been applied.
- **REQ-SHELL-145**: Theme tokens carry these guarantees, each measured against every background the token is actually painted on: every state text colour (running, waiting, done, info, fail), the primary, secondary and tertiary text colours, and the readable accent shade clear 4.5:1 on every text background; strong borders and the focus colour clear 3:1 on every boundary background; a primary button's label stays at or above 4.5:1 in its rest, hover and pressed states; disabled text is dimmer than enabled text — below half the enabled contrast ratio — while still clearing 3:1; every persona base colour and every shade given to a concurrently running instance clears 4.5:1, and no two concurrently running instances are given the same shade.
- **REQ-SHELL-146**: Auxiliary windows meet the same contract independently of the main sweep: the terminal window has all controls named and no contrast violations both while a command runs and after it exits, and its empty state is announced and explained.
- **REQ-SHELL-147**: Every dropdown in every primary view sizes itself to its selected entry (over-long values may elide visually but are announced in full); a dropdown must never visually swallow its current value when space is tight.
- **REQ-SHELL-148**: Self-painted navigation controls draw the keyboard-focus ring entirely within their own bounds, at the theme's focus-ring width and color, so no part of the ring is cut off; divider handles take focus and are named (REQ-SHELL-068).
- **REQ-SHELL-160**: Four properties of this contract are settled from rendered output on a real display and not from theme tokens, and each has the acceptance criterion given. (a) **Contrast**: the ratios of REQ-SHELL-144 and REQ-SHELL-145 are measured from the pixels actually painted, sampling the rendered label against the rendered background behind it after all styling, states and overlays have been applied — a token pair that computes correctly but paints over a different surface fails. (b) **Native surfaces**: on each supported platform, and in each built-in theme, the native dialogs, menus, text caret and disabled text of REQ-SHELL-130 are inspected on screen; the criterion is that no native surface presents a light background while a dark theme is active, or the reverse, and that text on those surfaces still clears 4.5:1. (c) **Focus indication**: on each supported platform every focusable control of REQ-SHELL-148, including self-painted navigation controls and divider handles, shows a focus ring that is wholly within the control's own bounds and clears 3:1 against the surface it is drawn on. (d) **Scaling**: the icon rule of REQ-SHELL-030 is measured at 100%, 125%, 150%, 175% and 200% OS display scaling, and again after the window is dragged between two monitors set to different scale factors; the criterion is that the icon covers its declared logical size within one device pixel in each case and is never drawn from artwork smaller than the required raster. A build that cannot demonstrate all four does not meet this domain's accessibility contract.
- **REQ-SHELL-149**: State is never conveyed by color alone anywhere in the shell: the session chip carries the status word beside its dot; the take-control button re-labels for its state; transient acknowledgement kinds carry the word-form accessible names REQ-NOTIFY-019 fixes; the active theme is shown as text; keycap sequences announce the written chord; demo-mode and service-unavailable refusals use explicit messages — exactly "Artifacts are read-only in demo mode.", "Artifact not found in the loaded session.", "Proposal actions are unavailable in demo mode.", "Settings are read-only in demo mode.", "Worktree integration is unavailable in demo mode.", and "Runs are unavailable in demo mode."

### Update responsiveness

- **REQ-SHELL-150**: A rapid succession of changes never makes the shell unresponsive and never leaves it showing stale content. A single isolated change is visible without perceptible delay. While changes keep arriving, the affected surface is redrawn at least once every 120 ms, and after the last change of a burst the surface shows that final change. No intermediate state of a burst is required to be drawn. These are the numeric acceptance thresholds, all measured while the shell is under a sustained burst of changes at a rate the display cannot match: a user gesture — a keystroke, a click, a view switch, a divider drag — is acknowledged on screen within **100 ms** of the gesture; the interval between consecutive repaints of a surface that has pending changes never exceeds **120 ms**; and the final state of a burst is on screen within **120 ms** of the last change arriving. A build that misses any of the three under sustained load fails this requirement, whatever its average behaviour.
- **REQ-SHELL-151**: A surface that is not on screen — a hidden or closed window, or a panel behind another view — consumes no redraw work while it is not on screen, and shows fully current content the instant it becomes visible: it never presents a stale frame first, and no catch-up redraw is visible after it appears. This holds for the persona windows, for the terminal window, and for the shell's panels. The acceptance criterion for "consumes no redraw work" is stated as work rather than as a CPU share, because it must be checkable on any hardware: while a surface is off screen, zero repaints of that surface are performed and zero repaint or refresh timers belonging to it are running, however fast changes arrive for it. When it becomes visible again, its first painted frame already carries the current state, and it reaches that visible state within the **100 ms** gesture budget of REQ-SHELL-150.

### Tab strips and vertical navigation lists

- **REQ-SHELL-152**: Tab strips throughout the shell behave alike. When a strip first appears, its first tab is the active one. From the keyboard, Left and Right move to the neighbouring tab and select it in the same step, Home selects the first tab and End the last, and movement stops at either end rather than wrapping around. Switching tabs exchanges panes that are already built, so no tab shows an intermediate blank state. A selection restored from a stored key the strip does not contain leaves the tab that is currently active still active — for example, a stale `interface/settingsTab` value falls back to the models category — and never clears the selection. A selection the user did not make produces no effect beyond showing that tab's pane.
- **REQ-SHELL-153**: Vertical navigation lists throughout the shell behave alike. Arrow Up, Arrow Down, Home and End move focus without changing the selection, and selecting requires Space or Enter, so moving focus across entries never causes the passed-over destinations to be shown. The selected entry is marked by a rule down its left edge, and its accessible description says so in words: "Selected, marked with a rule down its left edge." for the selected entry and "Not selected." for the others. Entry glyphs are sized from the theme's type scale, so icons grow when text does. The main window's navigation rail is not a vertical navigation list in this sense: it follows the button semantics of REQ-SHELL-027 through REQ-SHELL-032.

### Miscellaneous shell behaviors and edge cases

- **REQ-SHELL-154**: Artifact detail dialogs are per-artifact windows: a repeat request for the same artifact raises the open dialog instead of opening a second one; in demo mode the request is refused with "Artifacts are read-only in demo mode."; an unknown artifact identifier answers "Artifact not found in the loaded session." All artifact dialogs close on window close and on new session.
- **REQ-SHELL-155**: Restoring a checkpoint while a run is live is refused, and a run whose liveness cannot be determined counts as live for this purpose, so the restore is refused there too. The refusal SHALL be reported on the transient error notification of REQ-SHELL-112, the same surface the workspace-open and new-session refusals use, and SHALL convey three things: that nothing was restored; that the reason is a live run, or that liveness could not be read and is therefore treated as live; and that finishing or cancelling the run makes restore available. The wording is left to the implementation. A refused restore SHALL change nothing at all — no file, no checkpoint record, no view contents — and SHALL leave the checkpoint listing exactly as it was, so a refusal is never a partial restore. **That refusal is a condition holding for the whole of the restore and not a test taken once**, on the terms REQ-SESSION-164 fixes; this requirement holds no copy of them and states no window of its own. What follows here is only what the shell owes: a restore refused because a session became live after the question was answered SHALL be reported on the same surface, in the same terms, as one refused at the moment it was asked — the user is not shown a different kind of failure according to when the refusal was reached. A session recorded as running whose process is no longer present has its recorded status corrected, and the correction is announced through a WARNING notice titled "Session marked interrupted". **That title and that shape are the SINGLE-session case, and a repair that corrects several sessions at once is announced once rather than once each** (accepted amendment 2026-09-06, OQ-46). One repaired session SHALL keep the title above word for word. A batch SHALL raise exactly one WARNING notice that conveys how many sessions were repaired, names them, and carries an action routing to the surface the affected-session list is reached from; its own title is left to the implementation, subject to being distinguishable from the singular one. The action SHALL be a member of REQ-NOTIFY-044's closed vocabulary and this amendment adds none to it. The realistic failure is plural rather than exceptional — a run-host crash stales one session per in-flight run, and concurrent runs are a supported shape (REQ-MONITOR-249 to REQ-MONITOR-258) — so five notices saying the same thing about five identifiers is not a report of it. **Every repaired session still receives its own timeline entry** (REQ-SESSION-062); this requirement governs what is shown and never what is recorded. **Continuation is offered only once the relevant batch has settled**: no session of a batch may be offered for continuation while another session of that same batch is still being repaired, so the list the user acts on is never a partly repaired one. At startup the window requests the checkpoint listing for the session that is selected first.
- **REQ-SHELL-156**: While the checkpoint backend is busy, a checkpoint-listing request retries every 150 ms with a single pending retry that always targets the latest requested session — clicking quickly from one session to the next cannot accumulate queued retries.
- **REQ-SHELL-157**: Prompt history and stash persist per workspace and survive a restart with no action by the user; where they are held is REQ-LIBRARY-138's and this document names no location. No partly applied change to either list is ever observable, and no leftover working state is left behind by a save. A failed save raises the persistent notice "Could not save prompt history" — "Rotaris will keep prompts in memory for this window." — and prompts continue to work in memory for that window.
- **REQ-SHELL-158**: When the most recently polled cloud-credit balance shows no credit, starting a run raises a confirmation offering "Start anyway". It is raised on either condition, and either alone is enough: the polled balance is at or below zero, or that same poll reported the service declining new work (admission not allowed, REQ-PROVIDER-130). The two need not agree, and no reconciliation between them is attempted. The confirmation's title and body SHALL convey three things — that the account has no credit or is being refused new work; that a run on a cloud model may therefore fail partway or not start at all; and that the choice is between starting anyway and topping up — with their wording left to the implementation. It offers exactly two answers: "Start anyway", and a declining answer whose own label states that it opens the account page; the declining answer is the default. The confirmation is answered from that already-polled balance: no network request is made between the user asking for the run and the confirmation appearing, so the confirmation cannot be delayed by a slow or unreachable cloud service. Choosing "Start anyway" starts the run; choosing the declining answer starts no run and opens the cloud account page in the default browser; dismissing the dialog with Escape starts no run and opens nothing (REQ-SHELL-055).
- **REQ-SHELL-159**: A workspace's composer draft is stored under `workspaces/{workspace-key}/composerDraft` in the per-user preference store, with the empty string as its default, and is written with debouncing while the user types rather than on every keystroke: the write is issued 400 ms after the last keystroke, and is additionally flushed immediately — without waiting out the interval — whenever the composer loses focus, the active view changes, the workspace is replaced, or the window closes. Survival of an abnormal termination is deliberately not promised, and the bound is stated instead: a power loss or a killed process may lose at most the keystrokes of the last debounce interval, and nothing earlier, because every deliberate departure from the composer has already flushed. A draft is never left half-written: a write either lands whole or does not land, so the text read back is always text the user actually typed at some moment. The text standing in the composer when the workspace is closed is the text the composer holds when that workspace is opened again.
- **REQ-SHELL-167**: Every numeric constant stated in this document is normative and individually checkable, with exactly one class of exception: a value qualified "in the default theme" is a theme token (REQ-SHELL-129), where the requirement is that the value is read from the active theme and that the default theme declares the stated number — another built-in or future theme may declare a different one, and the layout must follow it. In particular the following are normative and not merely the current values: the auxiliary window sizes 820×620 (REQ-SHELL-072) and 900×620 (REQ-SHELL-081), which are the sizes the window opens at when the display work area can hold them, and otherwise the largest size within that work area preserving the stated aspect — the user may resize freely afterwards, and this document does not require an auxiliary window's size to be remembered; the elision caps of 28 characters on a terminal tab label, 12 on a background-session identifier (REQ-SHELL-082) and 90 on a command label (REQ-SHELL-087), each counted in characters as the user sees them and each keeping the full text in the tooltip and accessible description; and the responsive thresholds of 1180 px and 1080 px in the status strip (REQ-SHELL-047) and 1180 px for Workspace compact mode (REQ-SHELL-059); and the 650 ms deferred first-paint delay (REQ-SHELL-011). A build that misses any of these misses the requirement that states it. The transient acknowledgement surface contributes no constant to this inventory: this document states none of its figures, which belong to REQ-NOTIFY-011, REQ-NOTIFY-013 and REQ-NOTIFY-022 and are cited from REQ-SHELL-112 and REQ-SHELL-113.
- **REQ-SHELL-168**: This window hosts the run surfaces REQ-ORCH-155 closes, the plan gate of REQ-ORCH-153 and REQ-ORCH-154 among them. Each is presented inside the primary view of REQ-SHELL-023 that carries the run it belongs to, and every action such a surface offers is an entry of the one action vocabulary of REQ-SHELL-048, so it is presented identically on every surface that vocabulary reaches. What each of those surfaces must convey is fixed by the requirement that names it, and this document holds no copy of it; what this document contributes is that they are hosted here rather than anywhere else, that they are reached like every other action, and that they carry the shell obligations every surface carries (REQ-SHELL-149, REQ-SHELL-135, REQ-SHELL-022). The plan gate is the one surface in this document that no dismissal answers: window-level Escape (REQ-SHELL-055) does not answer it, and neither does switching view, closing the view, nor closing the window — it resolves only through the three answers REQ-ORCH-153 fixes, because any other route would be the default answer that requirement forbids. A session waiting at that gate is consequently not an active run for the third close guard of REQ-SHELL-119 — it is waiting on the user rather than executing, and presents as paused under REQ-NOTIFY-049 — so closing the window raises no run-active prompt on its account, ends nothing, and leaves the decision still pending when that session is opened again.
