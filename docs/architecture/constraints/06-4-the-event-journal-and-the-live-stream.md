# Architectural constraints extracted from the specification — 4. The event journal and the live stream

Part 6 of 22 of [the constraints](../constraints.md), which indexes every part.

## 4. The event journal and the live stream

| # | Constraint | Label | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-01 | **Re-derived: the byte-identity survived; the file it was a property of did not.** `REQ-EVENTS-079` and `REQ-EVENTS-093` are **withdrawn** — the line-delimited form "moves whole to the export". What survives untouched is that a recorded event is **byte-for-byte what the live stream emitted** — same field names, same schema version, same key ordering — which ADR-0009 discharges by storing the serialised wire line **verbatim** as the record's payload and never rebuilding it from query columns. Replaying and subscribing therefore remain interchangeable, and that equality is now the conformance test of E-17 rather than a structural fact. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-080, REQ-SESSION-143; REQ-EVENTS-079 and REQ-EVENTS-093 withdrawn |
| E-02 | Serialization is compact JSON, keys sorted, non-ASCII escaped; two equal events serialize to byte-identical lines; a line containing a carriage return or line feed is refused rather than recorded. **The terminator rule is re-anchored, not weakened**: `REQ-EVENTS-090` now governs "any line-delimited **rendering** of a history", says a single line feed is the record terminator on every platform "so that the same history exported on two platforms is byte-identical and equals the bytes the wire carried", and states in terms that **"a store that does not separate its records by newlines does not violate it"**. It is the export's format. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-064, REQ-EVENTS-065, REQ-EVENTS-089, REQ-EVENTS-090, REQ-SESSION-145 |
| E-03 | **Re-derived into two store-neutral observables.** `REQ-EVENTS-029` no longer says "one write followed by an immediate flush". It requires each event added as **exactly one record, made durable individually at the moment it is raised and never in accumulated batches**, and states the two things that buys: the durable record `REQ-EVENTS-072` binds an event to is written and readable at the moment the event can be observed on any channel — so a run killed between the two leaves a record with no event and never an event with no record — and a session abandoned by a crash holds its history complete to the last event raised. Delivery to a live consumer is likewise per event, never batched. *`REQ-EVENTS-029` still carries a stale cross-reference to "the debounced state snapshot [which] may lag by its whole window"; that clause's conclusion is false under `REQ-SESSION-067` and is reported as a specification defect.* | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-029, REQ-EVENTS-072 |
| E-04 | Appending **never raises**. A serialization failure drops that one event with a warning; a write failure or short write **marks the store failed for the remainder of the run** and leaves a partial last line in place, never repaired by a reader. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-032, REQ-EVENTS-088, REQ-EVENTS-100 |
| E-05 | The channel and the store are established **before any part of run start-up can fail** and closed unconditionally after the run; closing flushes, is idempotent and must not damage the file. **New clause, minted 2026-09-02 against a measured gap: "established" now means EXERCISED, not merely opened.** `REQ-EVENTS-179` requires the store to be established as **usable** before the run starts, and states that nothing the store reports about itself is sufficient evidence — it may report the open a success, report itself writable, return every setting read back exactly as applied, create the further files it keeps alongside itself at their expected sizes, **and still fail on the next operation**, and may fail on that operation every time while everything after it succeeds. The check MUST be an operation that reads and writes the store's own contents — a write made and then undone, so nothing is added — and MUST NOT be a reading back of what the store says about its state. It sits where this row puts establishing the store and runs **before** `REQ-EVENTS-087` creates the history. Where it fails, the store counts as one that could not be opened and **`REQ-EVENTS-084` governs unaltered** — refusal on `REQ-EVENTS-021`'s terms, no `session.start`, no session left behind, the store's own reason unaltered. **The failure MUST NOT be retried into success, swallowed or reduced to a warning: exactly one attempt, its outcome decides, and a store that fails and then works still refuses the run.** *That last clause is the one an implementer will soften, because retrying makes the symptom go away — 30 of 30 opens threw and 6,000 of 6,000 commits then succeeded — and a product that retries runs on storage it has already been told is unsound, with no later moment at which it can say so.* **This closed a real hole rather than tightening a good boundary:** `REQ-EVENTS-084` governed a store that cannot be **opened** and `REQ-EVENTS-088` a write failing **during** a run, and the measured case belonged to neither, 30 times out of 30. Both requirements gained the boundary sentence that routes to `REQ-EVENTS-179`, and `REQ-SESSION-089` gained the matching clause on the run-lifecycle side. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-034, REQ-EVENTS-035, REQ-EVENTS-087, REQ-EVENTS-179, REQ-EVENTS-084, REQ-EVENTS-088, REQ-EVENTS-021, REQ-SESSION-089; `spikes/remote-and-synchronised-storage.md` §4.2–4.3, §9 |
| E-06 | **Re-derived.** Retention: the cap is `runtime.event_retention_per_session` (default 20000, `0` never trims and carries a null cap into the truncation record, negative rejected at configuration load so it never reaches a store); headroom is cap divided by 8; recording an event that takes the history past `cap + headroom` MUST be followed by a trim **before any further event is recorded**; the trim is **observable only as a single step**; an incomplete trim leaves the previous contents in force, and the history sitting over its cap is a bounded and visible cost rather than a failure. *`REQ-EVENTS-091` does not say the trim shares the append's durable step and `REQ-SESSION-133` does say it for the rings — which is where ADR-0009 and ADR-0004 differ, stated in both rather than smoothed.* | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-091, REQ-CONFIG-473 |
| E-07 | **Re-derived: the file, its byte format and the corrupt-marker branch are gone; the four facts and two rules survive.** The truncation record carries its four facts, **dropped counts accumulate across store instances and processes** for one session id and are **never reset**, and absence reads as zero drops. The corrupt-marker-reads-as-untruncated branch has no referent. **One clause got stronger**: `REQ-EVENTS-092` now requires the truncation record made durable **in the same indivisible step as the trim that produced it**, which closes a crash window the file design accepted. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-092, REQ-EVENTS-094 |
| E-08 | A resumed session **continues the same history**, so one history may hold several start-to-result sequences. *(The file this row named has no referent; the continuation rule is unchanged.)* | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-097 |
| E-09 | Ordering within a run is fixed in full, judged alike by live delivery order and by line position. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-016, REQ-EVENTS-020, REQ-EVENTS-069 – REQ-EVENTS-071, REQ-EVENTS-076, REQ-EVENTS-083 |
| E-10 | A run **refused before it starts** emits exactly one `result` with status `error` and no `session.start`, by a direct path; a run that started delivers its terminal pair over the bus. **The two paths are mutually exclusive.** | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-021, REQ-EVENTS-070, REQ-SESSION-082, REQ-SESSION-177 |
| E-11 | **Masking is a property of the event definition**, re-applied when a field is assigned after construction; the store applies no second pass and the exporter applies none either. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-040, REQ-EVENTS-059, REQ-EVENTS-095, REQ-EVENTS-134 |
| E-12 | Every event carries four envelope fields and **every payload field listed for its type — a field may be null or empty but must not be absent**. A validating consumer must reject none of a completed run's events. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-017, REQ-EVENTS-036, REQ-EVENTS-041 |
| E-13 | Event schema version is `1` and is versioned **independently** of the session snapshot version. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-038, REQ-EVENTS-039 |
| E-14 | Reading is streaming, lazy, read-only and forward-compatible: an unknown event type is returned with its raw payload intact and a flag, never dropped and never raised; the recognized set is derived from the catalogue itself, never a second list; damaged lines skipped with exactly one warning each. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-099 – REQ-EVENTS-107 |
| E-15 | A stored record must be able to **re-render itself byte-identically** from its stored payload. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-104, REQ-EVENTS-172 |
| E-16 | **The catalogue is closed at 23 types and is deliberately not extended** for the verification-gate log, whose detail stays in its own evidence file. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-041, REQ-EVENTS-177 |
| E-17 | **Re-derived: the amendment landed and it went further than this row asked for.** `REQ-EVENTS-166` is now written as **the obligation that makes a store-only history acceptable, discharged by export**, and declares itself **"the single definition of what an export of a session record must guarantee, for this document and for every other"** — two forms (`REQ-EVENTS-172`, `REQ-EVENTS-132`) and three attached obligations, including the integrity carry-over. E-02's serialisation rules are re-anchored there by `REQ-EVENTS-090`. `REQ-SESSION-199` extends the same guarantee to the **timeline, issues record, metrics document and permission audit**, with three further obligations of its own: it is **not a channel**, the audit **exports as an audit** (every entry in decision order, none omitted, and a statement that entries were dropped and how many), and it **exists to be checked against**, so an export that summarised, reordered or filtered what the surface shows would defeat the only reason it exists. `REQ-EVENTS-162` is promoted from a cross-check to **the** conformance test and must run on every build. **What the export list does not yet cover is a gate item, not a note:** SR-9(a) ruled improvement artifacts and delivery records in on the same terms and **neither is minted** — domain 14 contains no export requirement at all — while `REQ-TOOLS-190` excludes the verification-gate record by a positive sentence the identical argument reaches. And `REQ-SESSION-136`'s tool-call evidence must **never** gain one, because it deliberately holds unredacted arguments where `REQ-TOOLS-079` gives an audit entry none. | HARD | REQ-EVENTS-166, REQ-EVENTS-162, REQ-EVENTS-172, REQ-EVENTS-132, REQ-SESSION-199, REQ-SESSION-191, REQ-TOOLS-190, REQ-SESSION-136 |
| E-18 | **Withdrawn with the requirements that fixed it.** `REQ-SESSION-149` and `REQ-EVENTS-108` to `REQ-EVENTS-116` are withdrawn in the specification under SR-2: the resumable byte-offset read, the restart flag and its three trigger conditions, and the truncation marker's dropped-count comparison have no follower left to serve. **What replaces the job this row did** — detecting that a surface has fallen behind — is `REQ-MONITOR-214`'s per-session sequence number with `REQ-MONITOR-226`'s immediate reconcile (U-15), which detects on the very next publication rather than on the next scheduled read. **The marker's data survives**: `REQ-EVENTS-111` is explicit that the marker is not withdrawn, and E-07's accumulated dropped count and its four consumers stand. | WITHDRAWN | REQ-SESSION-149, REQ-EVENTS-108 – REQ-EVENTS-116 |
| E-19 | **Withdrawn in part.** `REQ-SESSION-160`'s placeholder row for a gap, and the rule that a re-seed replaces displayed rows in a single step, are withdrawn with the follower they served. **`REQ-EVENTS-052` is not withdrawn and is load-bearing in three more places than it was:** a row gets exactly two events — one when it first appears, one when it reaches its final form, and none for any state in between — indices are contiguous from 0, and a republished index **replaces** rather than appends. That contract is what makes reattach idempotent (ADR-0010), what bounds the persist-before-publish carve-out (D-16), and what decides when ADR-0008 writes the durable transcript row. **The purpose the placeholder served survives too**, in `REQ-MONITOR-224` and `REQ-SESSION-161`: declining an increment must not interrupt the user or blank the transcript, and the rows on display are unchanged by the increment that was declined. | HARD (`REQ-EVENTS-052`) / WITHDRAWN (`REQ-SESSION-160`) | REQ-EVENTS-052, REQ-MONITOR-224, REQ-SESSION-161 |
