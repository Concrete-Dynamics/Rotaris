### PD-SHELL-016 — the navigation is a sidebar holding the views and the sessions; the window opens on the Workspace

1. **The order.** The six primary views of [`PD-SHELL-015`](pd-shell-015.md#pd-shell-015) keep their identifiers and labels and take this fixed order: Workspace, Overview, Requirements, Git, Library, Settings. Workspace and Overview change places; nothing else moves.
2. **The sidebar.** The navigation beside the active view is a sidebar. Open, it is a column of `--rt-sidebar-width` that shows, top to bottom: a control that opens the start-session dialog (REQ-SHELL-125), labelled "New session"; Workspace and Overview under no heading, the two faces of the session in hand; the sessions list of clause 3 under the heading "Sessions"; then Requirements, Git and Library under the heading "Project"; and Settings at the foot, the spare height standing above it. A view's button shows its icon beside its label. A heading is text and takes no focus. Everything REQ-SHELL-028 states about a view button stands as written: the buttons are mutually exclusive, each is its own tab stop, the tooltip and accessible description are "Open {Label}", and click, Space and Return switch the view. The selection fill is the active view's alone.
3. **The sessions list.** The sidebar lists this workspace's sessions as `session.list` returns them, at most twelve, each with a state dot, its label on a line of its own, and under it either what it is doing ("running", "needs you") or when it last moved. Activating a row focuses that session and shows the Workspace, as the Overview's sessions card does (REQ-MONITOR-171). The followed session's row is marked by its ink and weight and by `aria-current`, never by the selection fill. "List sessions", REQ-MONITOR-164's label, opens the session browser (REQ-SHELL-126), which keeps every act on a session other than focusing it. The list is read when the window opens, when the followed session changes or stops being busy, and after each act of the browser. Sessions past the twelfth are counted under the list and reached through the browser.
4. **The startup view.** The view shown at startup is the Workspace. This rules [OQ-548](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/71-spikes-and-later-questions-38-continued-5.md#oq-548): a relaunch shows the Workspace, and no view is restored from the previous run of the application; REQ-TRACE-230's "restored as the active view after a restart" is read as it has been built, as the board's own state surviving and not the window's active view.
5. **The folded form.** Below a window width of 1600 px the sidebar is drawn folded unless the user opened it, and at any width a control at its foot folds and opens it. Folded, it is the rail REQ-SHELL-027 described: one button per view in clause 1's order with the icon above the label at REQ-SHELL-029's size, and the start control as an icon. In place of the list it shows one control under the start control, an icon shaped as that control is and never as a view, that opens the session browser, names the followed session in its tooltip and accessible name, and carries a dot while another session is running (the running colour) or needs the operator (the waiting colour, which outranks it). Every view button has the rail's width, so the selection is one shape on every view. The user's choice holds for the window's lifetime and is not stored.
6. **The followed session is named at every width.** The title bar's workspace chip carries the followed session's label beside the path (REQ-WORKSPACE-017's session half, which drew a dash), in the primary ink and the body face, so the folded form never leaves the operator without it.
7. **The shortcuts.** Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+6 open the six views in clause 1's order, so Ctrl+1 opens the Workspace and Ctrl+2 the Overview, under the labels "Open Workspace" and "Open Overview". Every other row of REQ-SHELL-049's table stands as written.

**Why, and what it costs.** Round 3 of [the information-architecture map](../../design/information-architecture.md), which holds the baseline, the three concepts weighed and the coverage map. On the built application at `2fb19824` a launch opened on a view whose body, with no session, is one sentence and a control that leaves it, while every run starts, is read and is steered on the Workspace, one navigation away. A session, the object every surface of the product is about, had no standing place: it was reached through a title-bar dialog, an Overview card or a header button, and none of the three was visible from the Workspace, Requirements, Git, Library or Settings. And the rail spent 68 px on six icons above 9 px labels and left the column under them empty at every window height.

[`PD-SHELL-015`](pd-shell-015.md#pd-shell-015)'s own critique refused this order and this startup view on 2026-11-11, on three grounds. Each is answered by clause 3 and not by argument. The ruling of [OQ-506](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/70-spikes-and-later-questions-37.md#oq-506) rested on the returning operator landing on the Overview's sessions card: that operator now meets the sessions list on the first screen and on every other one. "New session" already started work from the Overview: it now starts it from every view. And no usage data says whether a launch starts or resumes: the first screen now serves both in one step each, a prompt typed into the composer or a press on a session's row, so the question no longer decides the layout.

What a user can now do that they could not: type a prompt on the screen the window opens on; see and switch between the workspace's sessions from every view, with what each is doing; start a session from every view. What it costs: Ctrl+1 and Ctrl+2 change places, which the product can afford before its first release and which an operator who learned them must relearn once; the open sidebar takes 164 px more than the rail, so it is the default only from 1600 px, where the Workspace's transcript column is as wide as the rail left it at 1440; below that the operator sees the rail, the Sessions control and the session's name in the title bar, and opens the list with one press; the sessions list and the Overview's sessions card state the same set, the first to switch and the second to account, as [`PD-MONITOR-010`](pd-monitor-010.md#pd-monitor-010) says; and the fold is not remembered across launches.

> **Narrows:** REQ-SHELL-023, REQ-SHELL-026, REQ-SHELL-027, REQ-SHELL-029, REQ-SHELL-049

Its case on REQ-SHELL-023 and REQ-SHELL-049 is the order of the views and the two digits that follow it, and is disjoint from [`PD-SHELL-015`](pd-shell-015.md#pd-shell-015)'s, which is their count and Mission's retirement; that directive's clauses 1, 3 and 5 are superseded where they fix the order, the rail's presentation and the startup view, and stand in everything else. Its case on REQ-SHELL-027 is the open form's presentation; on REQ-SHELL-029, that the 9 px label is the folded form's and the open form's label is set at `--rt-text-sm`, neither ever elided; on REQ-SHELL-026, clause 4.
