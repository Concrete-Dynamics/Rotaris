**OQ-460 — May clause (4)'s second hand-over wait an interval of its own?** *(REQ-STARTUP-164, ADR-0055. Raised 2026-10-31 by the review of the instance's stand-down.)*

**What is open.** REQ-STARTUP-164 (3) allows "no waiting beyond that one interval", and (4) requires the launch to "attempt the hand-over once more". Where (2)'s hand-over already waited the full 5 s and the holder (4) reaches does not answer either, a bound on (4)'s attempt decides how long the launch runs, and the requirement states none of its own. ADR-0055 says "one interval" too.

**What the build proceeds under.** (4)'s hand-over is bounded by the same 5 s as (2)'s (`claimInstance`, `packages/platform/src/instance.ts`), so the resolution can take two intervals. The bound is still finite and no claim is added.

**What decides it.** A ruling on whether "that one interval" limits (2) and (3)'s wait alone, or the launch's whole resolution; under the second, (4) has only what remains of it, and a launch that used it all gets no real second attempt.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 by the owner ([R-090](../../product/requests.md#r-090)).** Kind reading, recorded as a directive because it departs from the requirement's words: "that one interval" bounds (2) and (3)'s wait alone, and (4)'s hand-over has an interval of its own, as built. [`PD-STARTUP-009`](../../product/directives/pd-startup-009.md#pd-startup-009) states it beside OQ-461's ruling, and REQ-STARTUP-164 carries the back-pointer.

**OQ-461 — What does a launch end as when the holder is live and never answers?** *(REQ-STARTUP-161, REQ-STARTUP-164, ADR-0055 consequence 14. Raised 2026-10-31 by the review of the instance's stand-down; [the note](../../defects/fixed/a-launch-whose-two-hand-overs-go-unanswered-ends-with-no-window.md), moved to `fixed/` on 2026-11-01.)*

**What is open.** Where the record has come to name another live claim that does not answer, the requirement pulls three ways. (4): the launch ends "having in either case started no rival". The sentence after it: "Every path therefore ends with exactly one instance running and a window on screen". Its first sentence: a record naming a process that cannot be reached "SHALL never leave the application unlaunchable". REQ-STARTUP-161 closes the outcomes at two, and `refused` is neither. (3) also lists "a process that is running does not answer" among the reasons to reclaim, without saying whether that holds for a record that came to name a different process during the interval.

**What the build proceeds under.** (3) leaves a record naming another live claim, on REQ-STARTUP-161's ground; the retry loses; (4) hands over once more; unanswered, `claimInstance` returns `refused` and main ends with exit code 2. One process runs, and no window is surfaced for this launch.

**What decides it.** A ruling on which sentence governs this path: refuse with no window as built, reclaim as (3)'s list reads and accept the brief second process, or some surface that tells the person what happened.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 by the owner ([R-090](../../product/requests.md#r-090)).** Kind change-behaviour: reclaim. A holder that is live and never answers is treated as one that cannot be reached — after (4)'s second hand-over goes unanswered within its interval, the launch reclaims the record and claims it a final time, and a claim that succeeds opens this launch's own window. The first sentence governs over (4)'s "SHALL then end"; the brief second process is the one the requirement already admits. Recorded as [`PD-STARTUP-009`](../../product/directives/pd-startup-009.md#pd-startup-009); not yet built — the repair is owed with the directive's first execution, and the note moves to `fixed/` with it.

**OQ-462 — What is the person told when a utility process is lost or never reports ready, and is it forked again?** *(ADR-0002, REQ-SESSION-066, REQ-NOTIFY-023, PD-MONITOR-002. Raised 2026-11-01 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091); [the note](../../defects/the-run-host-and-the-workspace-service-have-no-last-resort-exception-handler-and-end-without-a-word.md).)*

**What is open.** ADR-0002 says a run host that ends is not forked again and every run it held settles through stale detection; REQ-SESSION-066 states the two consequences the person must be able to observe afterwards, a stale marking in the list and a refused restore. No requirement says whether the person is told at the moment the process is lost, with what severity, or what main does with a child that never answers `ready` after `utilityProcess.fork`. The workspace service is forked again at the next open of its workspace; the run host never is, and a window whose run host is gone keeps its composer.

**What the build proceeds under.** A persistent notice of REQ-NOTIFY-023's shape at severity `error`, with no action, naming the process (the run host, or the workspace service with its workspace) and the exit status, raised from main's existing `exit` handlers; a child that misses a bound on `ready` (the run host's hello bound, applied to both) is ended and reported the same way; the run host is not forked again (ADR-0002), and the workspace service is forked again at the next open as today. The notice adds no member to REQ-NOTIFY-044's action vocabulary.

**What decides it.** A ruling on the notice and its severity, and on whether a lost run host may be forked again once the sessions it held are repaired (REQ-SESSION-060 to REQ-SESSION-063), which ADR-0002 rules out and the specification does not address.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) asked for the error notice with the run host's copy non-dismissable and an immediate refusal of every submit while the host is gone; Advocate B (the system's lens) asked for the same notice as a new PD-NOTIFY directive, a missed workspace-service `ready` refused on the open's own surface, and a named refusal of every later run-host request. Ruled as follows. (1) When the run host or a workspace service ends and `ChildrenEndedByMain` does not record main as having ended it and the application is not quitting, main raises one notice of REQ-NOTIFY-023's shape per loss event, never per session: severity `error`, `persistent` flag set (a dismiss is offered; REQ-NOTIFY-024's single slot makes a non-dismissable notice no guarantee of being seen, and honesty is kept by (3) instead), no action (REQ-NOTIFY-044 is closed; REQ-SHELL-155's repair notice already routes), an empty session identifier, a title naming the process (the workspace too, for a service) and telling "did not start" from "stopped", a body saying what follows (run host: runs in progress were lost, work saved up to REQ-SESSION-034's bound is kept, the sessions appear stale and are repaired per REQ-SESSION-066/182, no run can start until the application restarts; service: reopening the workspace restores it), and the exit status and pid in the technical detail. The trigger is "not ended by main", not "status ≠ 0" (PD-MONITOR-002's first execution: 0 is not proof of main's shutdown). (2) The run host's 5 s `hello` bound applies to the workspace service's first answer; a child that misses it is ended. The run host then gets the notice above; a service whose open is still pending is refused on the open's surface (REQ-SHELL-112), and raises no second notice. (3) After the run host's `exit`, every request main would send it is refused at once with a named refusal and headline, without waiting out the ProcessLink deadline (ADR-0002 11(b)); the composer draft is kept. (4) The run host is not forked again, before or after repair (ADR-0002 consequence 1); the specification's silence is recorded, and a change is the owner's. The workspace service is forked again at the next open. No directive: R-072 mints one only where a requirement is departed from, and none is. The build cites ADR-0002 consequence 1, REQ-NOTIFY-023, REQ-SESSION-066 and this ruling; its first execution reports into ADR-0002 and first-executions.md.

**OQ-463 — Should a credential at rest be encrypted under the platform's user-bound key, beyond the mode bits and the access-control list REQ-PROVIDER-016 states?** *(REQ-PROVIDER-016, REQ-PROVIDER-017, REQ-PROVIDER-018, REQ-PROVIDER-061, REQ-PROVIDER-134, ADR-0029. Raised 2026-11-01 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091).)*

**What is open.** REQ-PROVIDER-016 fixes the protection as `0700`/`0600` on POSIX and an explicit access-control list on Windows, read back; REQ-PROVIDER-017 fixes the file as a JSON object with five keys. Both hold today (`packages/providers/src/credentials/`). The platform offers more: Electron's `safeStorage` encrypts under a key bound to the signed-in user (DPAPI on Windows, the keychain on macOS, libsecret on Linux), so a copied file is useless elsewhere. An encrypted blob is not REQ-PROVIDER-017's JSON, and REQ-PROVIDER-061 and REQ-PROVIDER-134 rewrite bridge artifacts for external consumers on every save, which presumes a credential those consumers can read.

**What the build proceeds under.** Unchanged: the mode bits and the access-control list, as specified and as built. The hardening plan of R-091 builds nothing here; this question records the recommendation and its cost.

**What decides it.** A ruling on whether encryption at rest is wanted at the price of REQ-PROVIDER-017's file shape and the bridge artifacts' readability. A yes is a directive narrowing REQ-PROVIDER-017 and a later plan; a no closes the question with the reason.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended no, closing the question with reopening conditions written in; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended no, with the threat model and the revisit triggers written in. **Ruled no.** The credential at rest stays the file REQ-PROVIDER-017 states, under the protection REQ-PROVIDER-016 states and the atomic write of REQ-PROVIDER-018; no `safeStorage`, DPAPI, keychain or libsecret layer enters the credential path or the bridge artifacts. Three reasons ground it. First, ADR-0029 has already decided the substance: alternative A (a platform-held key with the file as a shim) is unavailable because REQ-PROVIDER-014, -017 and -019 make the path, the five keys, the indentation and the `*.json` listing observable, and consequence 1 records on purpose that the store protects against other users, not other processes of the same user. Second, the gain is smaller than it looks: REQ-PROVIDER-061, -063 and -134 require a plaintext copy of the same bearer token (the codex mirror, `access-token`, `api-key.json`) on disk before every save returns, for consumers outside the product's control (REQ-PROVIDER-064), so a copied data directory still yields the credential; the record's answer to the off-machine copy is placement, REQ-STARTUP-145 via REQ-PROVIDER-014 and -063. Third, the cost falls on the person: a lost DPAPI key, an unsigned macOS build (IC-3), or a Linux host with no secret service turns every credential into REQ-PROVIDER-017's "no credential" with no reason shown. A yes is the credential store's redesign, which R-091 lists as the owner's; a no keeps the specification and the build unchanged. **Reopen as a new request** when the bridge artifacts are retired, a requirement names same-user or offline-copy theft as a threat the product answers, or signed builds exist on every platform; the proposal must then state what the person sees when decryption fails. The build changes nothing: `hardening.md` §4 reads "ruled not wanted (OQ-463)" in place of "recommended, not built".

**OQ-464 — Does the workspace write boundary bind the terminal, git and MCP tools, or the file tools alone?** *(REQ-TOOLS-129, REQ-TOOLS-130, REQ-TOOLS-132, REQ-SANDBOX-001, ADR-0022. Raised 2026-11-01 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091).)*

**What is open.** `checkFence` (`packages/agent-tools/src/tools/fence.ts`) is "the ONE workspace-boundary check": the file tools resolve the real path and refuse one outside the root unless `runtime.allow_outside_workspace` is on, and `write-scope.ts` fences `write_file` and the edit tool. A shell command writes wherever the operating system allows, judged by the gate's command rules (`policy.ts`, `scanner.ts`) by shape and not by path; an MCP server writes where it will; OS confinement (ADR-0022) draws the boundary for every tool where it exists, and on native Windows none exists (IC-8). The requirements state the fence for the file tools and the confinement for the run, and say nothing about the terminal and MCP where confinement is absent.

**What the build proceeds under.** As today: the fence on the file tools, the gate's policy on commands, confinement where available, and `allow_unsandboxed_autonomous` gating autonomy without it. The hardening plan of R-091 hardens the fence it has (case folding only where the file system folds, no check-then-write gap) and widens nothing.

**What decides it.** A ruling on whether the specification's boundary is per tool or per run: under the second, a command whose arguments name a path outside the workspace is asked or refused by the gate on that ground, and an MCP tool with a filesystem scope is declared.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended a per-run boundary — path arguments sniffed out of every terminal command, asked under `ask`/`restricted` and refused under `autonomous`, plus a declared filesystem scope per MCP server; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended a per-tool boundary with no widening, one invariant test, and disclosure wording. The ruling is **per tool, covered**: REQ-TOOLS-129 fences file access by resolved real path; REQ-TOOLS-130 limits the opt-out to "the file tools and the permission gate's workspace-root scoping"; REQ-TOOLS-018 defines that scoping over the seven path keys alone and says a call carrying none "can never satisfy a path-scoped rule", which excludes parsing `command`; REQ-TOOLS-044 forbids any path-scoped ask under `autonomous` and admits only the five destructive deny rules; REQ-GIT-143 already fences `git_commit`'s `files` (`Path escapes workspace`). A heuristic shell-path check would be the opportunistic partial confinement REQ-SANDBOX-048 rejects by name, and an MCP scope declaration is a vocabulary no requirement has. For the terminal and MCP, ADR-0022's confinement is the run-wide boundary where available; on native Windows (IC-8) there is none, and both advocates leaned on `runtime.allow_unsandboxed_autonomous`, which PD-TOOLS-002 made inert — nothing gates it today, and this ruling does not pretend otherwise. REQ-SANDBOX-001 decides nothing here (egress vocabulary only). The build keeps D3 at its planned scope, adds one invariant test that every first-party tool whose schema declares a REQ-TOOLS-018 path key calls `checkFence` (git tools included), records in the hardening plan that the terminal and MCP are bounded only by confinement where it is available, and raises a follow-up open question on the wording of REQ-WORKSPACE-022, REQ-TOOLS-131 and REQ-CONFIG-327 ("any call") where confinement is absent — verbatim strings change by directive, not here.

**OQ-465 — May the per-session log be bounded in size, and by what shape?** *(REQ-SESSION-142, REQ-MONITOR-238, REQ-STARTUP-133. Raised 2026-11-01 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091).)*

**What is open.** REQ-SESSION-142 makes the per-session debug log "the session's log, which is a file so that a person can open it in an ordinary text reader", at the configured level, default WARNING; it states no bound. A run at DEBUG for hours appends without limit (`apps/desktop/src/run-host/tools.ts`, `appendFileSync`). REQ-STARTUP-133 bounds the diagnostics streams at four segments of 8 MiB, the oldest replaced: a precedent for a bound, in another mechanism.

**What the build proceeds under.** Unbounded, as today. The hardening plan of R-091 (bundle `log-sinks`) builds a bound only as this question is ruled, and otherwise leaves the log as it is.

**What decides it.** A ruling on whether a bound is wanted, and its shape: REQ-STARTUP-133's segments (the log becomes up to four files), a single file truncated at the head, or none.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended one file cut at the head in blocks of about 8 MiB with a plain-text marker and logging stopped if a cut fails; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended one file compacted to its most recent 16 MiB with a marker in the log's own line format and a starting size read from disk. Ruled: the log is bounded, and it stays one file. REQ-SESSION-002 closes the session's place on disk to three kinds of file, REQ-SESSION-142 and REQ-MONITOR-238 make the log "a file" a person opens in a text reader, and REQ-SESSION-191 opens "that session's log" as one target, so REQ-STARTUP-133's four-segment shape is rejected for this log; its 32 MiB per-stream figure is taken, so the product's logs share one number. Unbounded fails open: at DEBUG a long run can fill the disk the store shares (REQ-SESSION-198); a bound fails closed, losing old debug lines and never the record. The shape is the system's advocate's, with the user's advocate's two refinements: `session.log` holds at most 32 MiB; when an append would pass it, the appender keeps the most recent 16 MiB from the first line boundary and rewrites the file in place (no sibling or temporary file in the session's place), prefixed by one line in the log's own format at WARNING naming the cap and the cumulative bytes dropped so far; the appender reads its starting size from disk when opened, since later runs append to the same file; a failed compaction never fails the run — the appender writes one WARNING to the application log and appends nothing more for that run. The cap is a named constant beside `fileAppender` in `apps/desktop/src/run-host/tools.ts`, not a configuration key. This drops lines REQ-SESSION-142 says MUST be written, so a directive is minted. The build (bundle D2 `log-sinks`) proceeds under the directive, with a unit test of `fileAppender` under an injected cap and a first-execution row.

**OQ-466 — Does the renderer stay on the `file:` scheme, or move to a privileged custom scheme?** *(ADR-0044, REQ-SHELL-023, REQ-STARTUP-122 to REQ-STARTUP-128. Raised 2026-11-01 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091).)*

**What is open.** The renderer is loaded with `loadFile` in a packaged build and from the development server otherwise; its Content-Security-Policy is the meta tag in `apps/desktop/src/renderer/index.html`. The Electron security checklist's item 18 recommends a custom scheme over `file:`, so that which files may load is restricted at the protocol, and the `grantFileProtocolExtraPrivileges` fuse exists to be disabled by an application that has moved. Nothing in the specification names the scheme; the updater's verified-apply path (REQ-STARTUP-122 to REQ-STARTUP-128) and the CSP would both change with it.

**What the build proceeds under.** `file:` stays, with `grantFileProtocolExtraPrivileges` enabled; the navigation guard of R-091's bundle `perimeter-guards` admits only the application's own `file:` origin and the development server's, every other fuse the checklist names is flipped, and the CSP meta tag stays as written.

**What decides it.** A ruling, taken together with the next packaged artifact the updater applies: a scheme change is a load-path change the updater's verification and the CSP must follow.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended moving to a privileged custom scheme as its own load-path change, timed with the next packaged artifact, with an origin-state inventory and a packaged first execution; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended staying on `file:` for this build and closing the real gap, that `isOwnPage` admits every `file://` URL, with one exact-path predicate. Ruled with the system's advocate. Nothing in the specification names the scheme: REQ-SHELL-023 fixes the seven views, and REQ-STARTUP-122 to REQ-STARTUP-128 verify and apply an artifact's bytes without naming how it loads its renderer, so the entry's premise that the updater's path changes with the scheme is withdrawn and the next packaged artifact is not held for this question. ADR-0044 names no scheme either. The renderer stays on `file:`, `grantFileProtocolExtraPrivileges` stays enabled because `loadFile` needs it, every other fuse A2 names is flipped (ASAR integrity and `onlyLoadAppFromAsar` among them), and the CSP meta tag stays as written. The build must define the application's own page once, as one exported predicate in `apps/desktop/src/main/permissions.ts`: a `file:` URL whose decoded, normalised path is the built renderer's `index.html` under the application's own renderer directory, or in development the dev server's exact origin; a sibling file, a traversal, an encoded traversal, another drive or a UNC path, or a URL that fails to parse is refused. The permission handlers (OQ-459), A1's `will-navigate`, `will-redirect` and `setWindowOpenHandler` guard, A1's sender-origin check in `runCommand` and B2's reload target all use it unchanged, with unit tests for each refusal above; A1's guard-refusal first execution reports against it. `hardening.md` §3.1 records checklist item 18 as deliberately not met under this ruling. A custom scheme, if wanted, is a new request with its own ADR note against ADR-0044 and ADR-0002, covering every load site, `registerSchemesAsPrivileged`, a handler serving only the asar renderer directory, the CSP, the predicate and the fuse.

**OQ-467 — What bounds the size of what a renderer command carries?** *(ADR-0044, REQ-CHAT-001, REQ-SHELL-020. Raised 2026-11-01 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091).)*

**What is open.** `CommandRequestSchema` (`packages/contracts/src/commands.ts`) caps attachments at ten and dictation audio at a stated size, and leaves `prompt`, every `path` and every name unbounded. A bound refuses a legitimate request past it; no bound lets a renderer hand main any size at all, and the renderer is the one process that shows web content. No requirement states a figure.

**What the build proceeds under.** Free text (`prompt` and its kin) at most 1,048,576 characters; a path at most 32,767 characters (the longest path Windows addresses); a name or identifier at most 1,024; a request past a bound is `command.malformed`, with a headline that says which field and which bound. The constants are named in `contracts`, and the composer is unchanged.

**What decides it.** A ruling on the figures, and on whether the composer refuses a prompt past the bound before sending it rather than main refusing it after.

**Ruled 2026-11-01 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended raising free text to 4,194,304 characters and a composer-side refusal before sending, with the draft's over-limit state shown; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended the provisional figures applied by field class, with `apiKey`, `baseUrl` and `url` in the path class, the arrays and `config.setField.patch` bounded, and the constants mirrored in `process-protocol.ts`. The ruling takes the system's advocate's figures and classes, and one point from the user's. No requirement states a figure, so the figures fill a gap: `FREE_TEXT_MAX = 1,048,576`, `PATH_MAX = 32,767`, `NAME_MAX = 1,024`, counted as JavaScript string length, named in `packages/contracts/src/commands.ts` and cited to this entry. Free text is every human-authored or content-bearing string (`prompt` in `session.start` and `session.prompt`, `composer.writeDraft.text`, `session.planEdit.text`, `session.planDecision`'s changes, `artifacts.save.body`, `prompts.push.text`); the path class takes every `path`, `attach.path`, `workspace`, `shell.openExternal.url`, `baseUrl` and `apiKey`, since a signed URL or a token can exceed a name bound; the name class takes every id, name, ref, branch, key, label and mimeType. `ids` arrays take a count bound of 1,024 and `config.setField.patch` a refine on its JSON length at `FREE_TEXT_MAX`; each `ImageAttachmentInputSchema.base64` is bounded provisionally at `DICTATION_AUDIO_MAX_BASE64`. The same constants bound `session.start` and `session.prompt` in `process-protocol.ts`, held equal by a test (ADR-0044's second boundary). A request past a bound is `command.malformed` returned as a value (ADR-0044 channel 5), its headline built from the first zod issue's path and maximum (for example "The prompt is longer than 1,048,576 characters"), never echoing the value (REQ-NOTIFY-005). The composer gains no pre-send check: REQ-CHAT-001 submits on Enter and REQ-CHAT-012 already restores the text and raises the reason; the build must keep attachments in place on that refusal and test it. A refused `composer.writeDraft` is not swallowed: the composer raises the returned headline in its existing error slot, which narrows REQ-CHAT-015 and REQ-CHAT-169 (PD-CHAT-011). REQ-SHELL-020 grounds the posture: main filters what a renderer asks. The 1 MiB figure refuses pastes a very-large-context model could accept; that is recorded here and is a later question's to raise, not the builder's.

**OQ-468 — Do the workspace-boundary sentences the user reads overclaim where OS confinement is inactive?** *(REQ-WORKSPACE-022, REQ-TOOLS-131, REQ-CONFIG-327, REQ-TOOLS-129, ADR-0022, IC-8. Raised 2026-11-01 by the ruling of OQ-464, which asked for it and did not rule it.)*

**What is open.** OQ-464 ruled the boundary per tool: the fence binds every first-party tool that takes a path, the gate's key scoping binds a tool call carrying a path key, and the terminal and MCP are bounded by OS confinement where it exists and by nothing on native Windows (IC-8). Three sentences the user reads say more. REQ-WORKSPACE-022 and REQ-TOOLS-131 state, verbatim, "File access: agents may read and write inside this workspace folder only." REQ-CONFIG-327 states that "any call reaching a path outside the workspace continues to require approval". Where confinement is inactive a shell command reaches any path the operating system allows, and the sentences promise what the mechanism does not hold.

**What the build proceeds under.** The sentences stay as the requirements state them; a change to those strings needs a directive, which OQ-464's ruling did not mint. The hardening plan hardens the fence (bundle `fence-hardening`) and adds OQ-464's invariant test, and changes no user-facing wording.

**What decides it.** A ruling on whether the wording is narrowed where confinement is inactive (a directive over the three requirements, stating the terminal's and MCP's boundary as it is), or left as the target's own words with the gap recorded in `docs/architecture/hardening.md`.
