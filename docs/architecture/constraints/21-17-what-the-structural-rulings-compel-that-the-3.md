# Architectural constraints extracted from the specification — 17. What the structural rulings compel that the specification does not (continued)

| W-31 | **New. A STORE IS ESTABLISHED WHERE NOTHING IS KEPT FOR THE WORKSPACE, AND NEVER WHERE A PLACE IS KEPT AND THE STORE IS MISSING FROM IT.** `REQ-SESSION-202` fixes the third state `REQ-SESSION-043` did not enumerate: a place for a workspace's durable session store exists and no store is in it. It reads as **a store that could not be opened** — resolving into `REQ-SESSION-089` and `REQ-EVENTS-084` and refusing the run on their terms — and **not** as "no store". **Three things this constrains that an implementation would otherwise get wrong.** *(1)* **The open path.** Most store bindings create on open; an implementation that opens by a call which creates when absent does not satisfy the requirement, because it will silently mint a fresh store over the condition and report the workspace as holding no sessions. ADR-0058's open sequence establishes that the store **works** and not that it **is the store this machine already had**, and those are different questions. *(2)* **The failure report.** For this one condition `REQ-SESSION-089`'s "the store's own reason, unaltered" has **no referent** — no store is present to give a reason — so the report must say what was actually found and a reason MUST NOT be synthesised to fill the slot. Any error shape assuming an engine-supplied reason needs a defined case for this. *(3)* **A fence, not a new surface.** Any action that establishes a store in a place holding none must be user-initiated and explicit: never automatic, never a default, never a side effect of starting a run, opening or preparing a workspace, or repairing anything else. **Why refusal rather than an empty list**, recorded so it is re-examined on its reasoning rather than re-litigated: guessing "no store" is undoable in effect — a fresh store is created, whatever was recorded becomes unreferenced, and the user is told by an empty list that their history is not here, which is the exact failure `REQ-SESSION-200` exists to prevent them discovering too late. **And the finding that makes this a constraint rather than a detail:** `REQ-EVENTS-179` is the specification's strongest statement that a store must be *proved* rather than trusted, and it **passes cleanly against a store that is not there**, because the write-and-undo it requires is answered perfectly by the empty store the attempt itself brought into being. The family's coverage now spans two domains and holds because `REQ-SESSION-202` says so from the sessions side. ***First built 2026-10-14 (fin43):*** *(1)* `openWorkspaceStore` refuses a place that exists and holds no `workspace.db` (`StorePlaceEmptyError`) and creates only where the place is absent, after OQ-266's cut-restore check in the same open; *(2)* the refusal, `workspace.store-missing`, names the directory and that the store is not in it, and no engine reason is synthesised; *(3)* nothing in the product establishes a store there — where the deliberate action lives is [OQ-281](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/59-spikes-and-later-questions-26.md#oq-281). ***Reported back 2026-10-14 (fin44, OQ-281 ruled under R-072):*** *(3)* holds as a fence and gains one deliberate door: the condition is resolved by the user in **Settings › Project › Session store** — the restore, admitted while the open holds `workspace.store-missing`, or a deliberate **"Start a new session store…"** beside it, Cancel the default, which says what was found and that it recovers nothing and keeps the found place moved aside under `<identifier>.displaced-<stamp>` — or by putting the store back in its place by hand and opening the workspace again. Neither action is reached by a run, an open, a preparation or a repair; the error notice only navigates there (`open-settings`, `project`). *(1)* gains its other half the same day: a first store is made whole in the dot-prefixed sibling `.<identifier>.new` and renamed into place, so the open itself never leaves a place without a store in it — a cut leaves the place absent; *(2)* reaches every refusal in that window: a start says nothing was started because the store could not be opened, and every other workspace-scoped command states the held condition, never "open a workspace".
