**OQ-493 — Does the accessibility contract REQ-SHELL-144..168 aim at a WCAG 2.2 conformance level, so that a success criterion it does not restate is owed, or is it the whole contract?** *(REQ-SHELL-144, REQ-SHELL-148, REQ-SHELL-160, REQ-SHELL-066, REQ-SHELL-068, REQ-SHELL-070, §1.11. Raised 2026-11-03 by [R-093](../../../product/requests.md#r-093), guide 08 §8 items 21 and 9 ([the guide](../../../research/design/08-platform-conventions-for-electron.md)), guide 09 §8 item 2 ([the guide](../../../research/design/09-accessibility-for-a-dense-desktop-tool.md)) and guide 10 §8 item 5 ([the guide](../../../research/design/10-ux-writing.md)) of the design research corpus and backlog rows 19, 46 and 102 ([the backlog](../../../research/design/backlog.md)); numbered from OQ-493: OQ-462 to OQ-492 are the hardening orchestration's.)*

**What is open.** No desktop requirement names WCAG: in `docs/spec/` the word occurs only in `17-terminal-ui.md`. REQ-SHELL-144 states its own thresholds (“4.5:1 for normal text, and 3:1 for large text … and for non-text boundaries”) and its own naming rule. REQ-SHELL-148 and REQ-SHELL-160 (c) draw the ring “wholly within the control's own bounds” at the theme's width. REQ-SHELL-066 to REQ-SHELL-070 move a divider by drag, by keys and by reset. §1.11 writes `612,480 tok`. Read against WCAG 2.2, guide 08 §8 D08-21 finds SC 2.5.7 (Dragging Movements, AA) unmet by a keyboard route alone. Guides 08 §8 D08-9 and 09 §8 D09-2 find a 2 px inset ring failing SC 2.4.13's area test (AAA). Guide 10 §8 D10-5 finds `tok` unexpanded under SC 3.1.4 (AAA). The guides argue SC 2.2.1, 2.2.2, 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 under OQ-498, OQ-497 and OQ-495. Whether any of these criteria binds depends on a conformance target the record never names.

**What the build proceeds under.** REQ-SHELL-144..168 as written, read as the whole contract: no WCAG success criterion binds unless a requirement restates it.

**What decides it.** One ruling naming the target: the contract as written; WCAG 2.2 AA, which makes each unmet AA criterion (2.5.7 now, and 2.2.1, 2.2.2, 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 where OQ-498, OQ-497 and OQ-495 find them unmet) a directive over the requirement it meets; or AA plus named AAA criteria (2.4.13 through a theme token under REQ-SHELL-129, 3.1.4 through an accessible expansion). The ruling also sets the weight OQ-498, OQ-497 and OQ-495 give their WCAG arguments.

**Ruled 2026-11-03 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended the contract as written with no conformance level, a WCAG criterion counting only where it names a harm the record leaves unanswered, and a reading that the token item's accessible description carries REQ-MONITOR-176's tooltip; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended the contract as written, WCAG 2.2 as guidance under §6.1, and the 2 px inset ring and the `tok` form standing. Ruled: REQ-SHELL-144..168 is the whole accessibility contract and names no WCAG conformance level; a WCAG 2.2 success criterion binds only where a requirement or directive restates it, and a guide's citation of one is weight behind a harm the record names, never a ground on its own (`docs/design/README.md` §6.1). Three grounds. REQ-SHELL-160 closes the contract in its own words — a build that cannot demonstrate its four properties "does not meet this domain's accessibility contract" — and REQ-SHELL-144, -145 and -148 state their own thresholds and geometry; the specification cites WCAG where it means to (REQ-TUI-025 borrows the 2.1 contrast formula and nothing else), so its silence in the shell is a choice. A level is wholesale by nature, and a directive governs "per identifier and never wholesale"; AA would reach PD-WORKSPACE-001's acknowledgement (OQ-498) and REQ-SHELL-066's 6 px grab area (SC 2.5.8) with no measurement and no proof behind either. The three criteria the entry names are answered inside the record. SC 2.5.7: not owed; REQ-SHELL-068 gives every divider a named focus stop, 16 px arrow steps and Home, and REQ-SHELL-070 a single-press reset, so the only operator left out is a pointer-only one with no on-screen keyboard, whom nothing in the record measures; a single-pointer resize is a new interaction and therefore a request, not a directive of this round. SC 2.4.13 (AAA): the 2 px inset ring of REQ-SHELL-148, REQ-SHELL-160 (c) and §1.8 note 3 stands; the width is a theme token (REQ-SHELL-129, REQ-SHELL-167), so another built-in theme may already declare a wider one without any ruling, and this ruling binds no forced-colours case (OQ-494). SC 3.1.4 (AAA): nothing is owed beyond what stands, because REQ-SHELL-039 and REQ-SHELL-058 already expose a strip item's tooltip sentence as its accessible description, and REQ-MONITOR-176 fixes that tooltip as the explanation that the figure counts tokens used by this workspace's runs — the expansion the guide asks for is the record's own; §1.11's `612,480 tok` governs the visible text unchanged, and a `KpiCard` reading `tok` with a tooltip exposes it the same way under REQ-SHELL-058, one without a tooltip owes nothing. OQ-495, OQ-497 and OQ-498 take their WCAG arguments as weight behind the harm their own requirements name and rule on that harm; a change they make is a per-identifier directive on the requirement the harm meets. No requirement is departed from; no directive. §1.11 and §1.8 each gain a dated reading note. What executes: `debug-ui` confirms on the status strip that the tokens item's accessible description is REQ-MONITOR-176's tooltip sentence (a defect under REQ-SHELL-039 if absent); nothing else is built. Reopened by an owner's request naming a conformance target (a procurement or distribution obligation is the owner's), or by a `review-ui`, `debug-ui` or autopsy finding of a pointer-only operator unable to size a pane, which reopens REQ-SHELL-068 alone as a request.

**OQ-494 — Under an active OS forced-colours palette, does the shell defer to it or switch to `high-contrast`, and what then marks a floating surface and governs REQ-SHELL-160 (b)?** *(REQ-SHELL-128, REQ-SHELL-160, §1.7. Raised 2026-11-03 by [R-093](../../../product/requests.md#r-093), guide 08 §8 item 12 ([the guide](../../../research/design/08-platform-conventions-for-electron.md)) and guide 09 §8 item 11 ([the guide](../../../research/design/09-accessibility-for-a-dense-desktop-tool.md)) of the design research corpus and backlog row 24 ([the backlog](../../../research/design/backlog.md)); numbered from OQ-493: OQ-462 to OQ-492 are the hardening orchestration's.)*

**What is open.** REQ-SHELL-128 offers three app themes, “rotaris-dim” (the default), “nocturne” and “high-contrast”. REQ-SHELL-160 (b) requires that “no native surface presents a light background while a dark theme is active, or the reverse”. §1.7 says “a shadow only ever means *this floats*”. Guides 08 §8 D08-12 and 09 §8 D09-11 note three effects of an OS forced-colours palette: it overrides every token, it may be light, and it removes shadows, so a transitory surface needs a 2 px border to read as floating. OQ-29's ruling read REQ-SHELL-160 (b) as covering only the surfaces the application tints, never an OS palette. The record says nothing of forced colours.

**What the build proceeds under.** The three app themes of REQ-SHELL-128, with no forced-colours handling stated.

**What decides it.** One ruling on the forced-colours stance: defer to the OS palette (forced-colors styles, a border on every floating surface, REQ-SHELL-160 (b) read for a light OS palette), or switch to `high-contrast` while one is active. Deferring widens the theme-proof matrix.

**Ruled 2026-11-03 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended deferring to the OS palette, a 2 px border in the system text colour on every floating surface, (b) read against the palette painted and a forced-colours case in the theme proof; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended deferring, a `--rt-border` hairline on every shadowed surface, (b) read under OQ-29 and no widening of the matrix. Ruled: while the OS reports forced colours active, the shell defers to that palette and switches no theme; the stored, selected and named theme stays the operator's. Three grounds. REQ-SHELL-131 commits a theme "at the moment it is made", REQ-SHELL-133 opens the dropdown "with the running theme already selected" and REQ-SHELL-149 shows the active theme as text, so an unasked switch rewrites the choice or names a theme not painted; `high-contrast` is `dark: true` (`themes.ts`) while a contrast theme is the operator's own palette and may be light; and painting the tokens back over an OS accessibility palette is what the setting exists to forbid. What marks a float is [§1.7](../../../design/README/01-1-the-design-language.md) read as written: "a 1 px hairline defines an edge; a shadow only ever means *this floats*". The build folds the hairline into the shadow: `--rt-shadow-md` and `--rt-shadow-lg` each open with a 1 px `--rt-border` ring, and `.dialog`, `.cmdk` and the narrow overlay drawers carry no border of their own, so when forced colours drop `box-shadow` those three lose their edge, while surfaces drawing a `border` keep theirs recoloured. Under forced colours every surface carrying `--rt-shadow-md` or `--rt-shadow-lg` therefore draws its hairline as a border at REQ-SHELL-129's hairline width, which the OS palette recolours; D09-11's 2 px is not adopted, since §1.7 fixes 1 px and nothing in the record shows it failing. The focus ring stands as OQ-493 left it: an outline is recoloured and kept; a ring drawn as a shadow would vanish and is a defect under REQ-SHELL-148. REQ-SHELL-160 (b) is read under [OQ-29](08-specification-questions-3.md#oq-29): it covers the surfaces the application tints, in each built-in theme; under a forced palette the application tints nothing and the palette is no built-in theme, so (b) is neither breached nor measured there, REQ-SHELL-130's `themeSource` from the dark flag stays set, and the theme-proof matrix keeps its three themes. No requirement is departed from; no directive; §1.7 gains a dated reading note. What executes: `debug-ui` adds the forced-colours hairline to the design system for the `Dialog`, the command palette and the overlay drawers, with guide 08's K9 under emulated `forced-colors: active` as the regression evidence. Which surfaces float is OQ-513's list; this ruling binds only what any of them carries under forced colours. Reopened by a `review-ui`, `debug-ui` or autopsy finding on a real contrast theme that a hairline does not read as an edge (§1.7's width alone), that a native surface contradicts the palette, or that a self-painting widget of REQ-SHELL-130 paints the theme's palette against a light one; by a macOS or Linux host exposing a forced palette; or by an owner's request for a theme that follows the OS.

**OQ-495 — Are renderer zoom and OS text enlargement supported inputs, when under them a 1000×680 window lays out below every compact threshold?** *(REQ-SHELL-022, REQ-SHELL-030, REQ-SHELL-160, PD-SHELL-003. Raised 2026-11-03 by [R-093](../../../product/requests.md#r-093), guide 09 §8 item 12 ([the guide](../../../research/design/09-accessibility-for-a-dense-desktop-tool.md)) and guide 14 §8 item 6 ([the guide](../../../research/design/14-how-a-surface-gives-way.md)) of the design research corpus and backlog row 5 ([the backlog](../../../research/design/backlog.md)); numbered from OQ-493: OQ-462 to OQ-492 are the hardening orchestration's.)*

**What is open.** REQ-SHELL-022 guarantees every view at “1000×680 logical pixels”. REQ-SHELL-160 (d) and REQ-SHELL-030 measure scaling only as “100%, 125%, 150%, 175% and 200% OS display scaling”, a change that leaves the logical width unchanged. Guides 09 §8 D09-12 and 14 §8 D14-6 read WCAG 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 as asking for text enlarged to 200 % and reflow under zoom. Renderer zoom at 200 % would lay a 1000 px window out at 500 CSS px, below REQ-SHELL-059's 1180 and REQ-SHELL-047's 1080. The record says neither whether zoom is reachable nor what it owes. PD-SHELL-003 revision 2 removed Electron's default application menu, and no code under `apps/desktop/src/` sets or limits a zoom factor.

**What the build proceeds under.** REQ-SHELL-022's 1000×680 guarantee under OS display scaling only, and REQ-SHELL-160 (d) as written.

**What decides it.** One ruling on whether renderer zoom and OS text enlargement are supported inputs. Yes owes a layout below 1000 CSS px (a directive over REQ-SHELL-022) and audits at those widths. No records both as outside the contract, and renderer zoom is then bounded or disabled.

**Ruled 2026-11-03 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended no, with a directive extending REQ-SHELL-022 that holds every window at a renderer zoom of 100 % and names OS display scaling and the screen magnifier as the only enlargement routes; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended no, as a reading of REQ-SHELL-022 at zoom factor 1 with no directive, a zoom route reaching the renderer being a defect against it. Ruled no, as a reading: neither renderer zoom nor OS text-only enlargement is a supported input. REQ-SHELL-022 states its minimum in "logical pixels", Electron's own unit, and the thresholds built on it (REQ-SHELL-047's 1180 and 1080, REQ-SHELL-059's 1180, REQ-SHELL-061's 850, REQ-SHELL-064's "within 1000 px", all normative under REQ-SHELL-167) are laid out in the same unit; the two coincide only at a zoom factor of 1, and REQ-SHELL-022 calls itself the product's "single definition" of the minimum, which a second, effective minimum of 500×340 would contradict. The record already names its enlargement routes: OS display scaling, which REQ-SHELL-030 and REQ-SHELL-160 (d) measure from 100 % to 200 % and which leaves the logical width unchanged, and the screen magnifier REQ-TRACE-310 names beside it. REQ-SHELL-049, the authoritative shortcut table, binds no zoom chord, and PD-SHELL-003 revision 2 removed the application menu whose roles carried them, so no requirement or directive offers zoom and none is departed from; no directive is minted, and §1 holds no sentence on scaling to amend. WCAG 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 weigh only behind the harm REQ-SHELL-022 names (OQ-493), and that harm — an operator whose window has collapsed into overlay drawers and an elided strip with nothing on screen saying why and no command back — is answered by keeping the guarantee unbreakable, not by moving it to 500 CSS px. From now the build proceeds under REQ-SHELL-022 read at zoom factor 1 in every window the application opens — the main window, a persona window (REQ-SHELL-072), the terminal window (REQ-SHELL-081) and each dialog, since zoom spreads across an origin's windows — and a route that changes any window's zoom factor (a keyboard chord, Ctrl+wheel, a pinch) is a defect against REQ-SHELL-022, repaired by holding that window's factor at 1 with a unit test beside `mainWindowOptions`. What executes: `debug-ui` drives the built app at 1000×680, tries each route in the main window, a persona window and the terminal window, reads the factor back, and on the Windows host raises the OS "Text size" setting above 100 % and reads REQ-SHELL-022's four properties on every primary view; the pass reports into first-executions.md. A work area that cannot hold the minimum at 200 % scaling stays OQ-508's, and an auxiliary window's smallest size OQ-527's. Reopened by a `debug-ui` or autopsy finding that OS text-only enlargement reaches rendered content and breaks a REQ-SHELL-022 property at 1000×680, which reopens REQ-SHELL-022 alone; by a Linux or macOS measurement (none exists, IC-3) showing its text scaling changes the laid-out width; or by an owner's request for in-product text size or zoom, a new control and therefore a request, not a directive of this round.

**OQ-497 — Does flipping the in-app reduced-motion setting stop a pulse or spinner that is already running, or only the next one to start?** *(REQ-SHELL-135, §1.8. Raised 2026-11-03 by [R-093](../../../product/requests.md#r-093), guide 11 §8 item 2 ([the guide](../../../research/design/11-design-critique-and-evaluation.md)) and guide 13 §8 item 8 ([the guide](../../../research/design/13-states-feedback-and-time.md)) of the design research corpus and backlog row 4 ([the backlog](../../../research/design/backlog.md)); numbered from OQ-493: OQ-462 to OQ-492 are the hardening orchestration's.)*

**What is open.** §1.8 permits `.status-dot.is-pulsing`, “used only while a state is actively running”, and the 0.8 s `.spinner`. REQ-SHELL-135 says the value “is read at each animation start rather than cached, so flipping the in-app setting … takes effect without relaunch”. An infinite animation that started before the flip has no later start at which to read it. Guides 11 §8 D11-2 and 13 §8 D13-8 read WCAG SC 2.2.2 as asking that self-starting motion running past 5 s beside other content can be paused, stopped or hidden, and a pulse may last a whole run.

**What the build proceeds under.** REQ-SHELL-135's resolution order, read at each animation's start.

**What decides it.** A reading of REQ-SHELL-135's “takes effect without relaunch”. Either a running pulse or spinner stops when the setting changes, so every StatusDot and Spinner re-reads it on change, or the change waits for each animation's next start. OQ-493's target sets the weight of the SC 2.2.2 argument.

**Ruled 2026-11-03 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended reading "takes effect without relaunch" as reaching every pulse and spinner on screen at the moment of the change, with a running state resuming its pulse when motion is turned back on; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended the same reading for the two endless animations §1.8 permits, with a transition already in flight finishing as built. Ruled as a reading: a change in the resolved reduced-motion value — the in-app setting flipped, or the OS signal flipped where REQ-SHELL-135 step (2) consults it — brings every running `.status-dot.is-pulsing` and `.spinner`, and every other running-state marker the shell animates while a state runs, to rest when the change happens: the dot at full opacity, the spinner as a static arc with no timer running; none waits for a new start, and each starts again, without relaunch, when motion is resolved on again and its state is still running. Three grounds. REQ-SHELL-135 puts the in-app and the OS leg in one sentence and promises the same effect of each, and the build already stops a running pulse on the OS leg through its live media query, so the in-app leg cannot be the weaker one without splitting that sentence; "read at each animation start rather than cached" forbids caching and does not license an animation to run on a value the operator has withdrawn — a read on change is the opposite of a cache, so the words stand unedited. REQ-SHELL-136 describes a state that holds while reduced motion is in force ("dots rest at full opacity", "no animation timer running"), as REQ-NOTIFY-022's "where reduced motion is in force" does, and a pulse that breathes for the rest of a run breaks it for hours. REQ-SHELL-137 already stops and restarts the spinner on a change it cannot predict ("only while the spinner is visible"). The resolved value governs on both legs: a stored "motion on" keeps motion on under an OS "reduce", by step (1), so a media-query block that stops a pulse against a stored choice is a defect of the build under REQ-SHELL-135, recorded in `docs/defects/` and repaired in the same pass. SC 2.2.2 is weight behind that harm, as OQ-493 fixed, and nothing more: whether the pulse is essential is not ruled, the in-app setting — reachable mid-run in the Settings view and applied at once under REQ-CONFIG-406, with the activity still stated in words under REQ-SHELL-149 and REQ-LIBRARY-125 — is the record's one means of stopping a running pulse, and a pause control beside it is a request, not this ruling's. A finite transition already in flight (an acknowledgement, a notice, a menu panel) completes as built, having read the value at its start, on OQ-496's ground; what the pulse marks is OQ-502's. No requirement is departed from; no directive is minted; [§1.8](../../../design/README/01-1-the-design-language.md#18-motion) gains a dated reading note. `debug-ui` executes it on every `StatusDot` and `Spinner` and every running-state marker stamped by `markMotionStart`, with one change notice from the resolved value that every stamp follows, a unit test beside `motion.ts`, and a free `app:drive` pass that flips the setting during a pulsing run and shows the dot at rest in the next shot and pulsing again after the flip back; the pass also records `Spinner`'s default name "Loading" against REQ-SHELL-137's "Working" as a defect. Reopened by a `debug-ui` or autopsy finding that the re-read cannot meet REQ-ORCH-155's 100 ms budget, or by an owner's request for a pause control separate from reduced motion; macOS and Linux OS signals mid-run stay unmeasured until a host exists (IC-3).
