# Desktop Shell, Navigation, and Window Management — Transient and persistent feedback surfaces

Part 4 of 7 of [domain 12](../12-desktop-shell-windows.md), which indexes every part. Defines REQ-SHELL-112 – REQ-SHELL-143.

### Transient and persistent feedback surfaces

- **REQ-SHELL-112**: The main window hosts exactly one transient acknowledgement surface, and it is the surface REQ-NOTIFY-013 defines. This document declares no acknowledgement surface of its own and holds no copy of its structure, its constants or its presentations: what the window relies on is REQ-NOTIFY-013 for there being one surface and for its bounded width, REQ-NOTIFY-011 for how long an acknowledgement stays on screen, REQ-NOTIFY-012 for its anchor, its margins and its drawing order, REQ-NOTIFY-014 for its neutral and error presentations and for their contrast against the surface behind them (REQ-SHELL-144), and REQ-NOTIFY-022 for its motion. Placement within the window is the one thing this document contributes to that definition: the host REQ-NOTIFY-012 anchors to is a Rotaris window rather than the screen, so the surface lives inside that window's content area and is carried by it rather than being a top-level surface of its own; for every acknowledgement this document raises, that host is the main window. Where a requirement in this document reports a refusal or a failure on "the transient error notification of REQ-SHELL-112" it names this one surface in the error presentation of REQ-NOTIFY-014.
- **REQ-SHELL-113**: The window has no second transient surface. The general toast stack is not a surface of its own: it is the single acknowledgement surface of REQ-SHELL-112 holding more than one member, stacked as REQ-NOTIFY-013 states, and every transient acknowledgement the window raises — one at a time or several at once — is shown there. This document holds no copy of the stack's depth, its widths, its lifetimes, its kinds, its glyphs, its accessible names, its close control, its focus behaviour or its motion: it relies on REQ-NOTIFY-013 through REQ-NOTIFY-022 for all of those, and on REQ-NOTIFY-003 for which events an acknowledgement may report on its own and which must additionally be published as the persistent notice of REQ-SHELL-114.
- **REQ-SHELL-114**: A single persistent notice at a time is surfaced by an inline banner under the title bar, carrying severity, title, message, optional copyable details (a copy control appears when details exist), an optional action (label plus action identifier), and a persistence flag. Dismissing clears the notice; dismissing an update-offer notice first records the offered version as waved-off (REQ-SHELL-118).
- **REQ-SHELL-115**: A banner action or an empty-state action performs exactly one of the following, and an action offered under the same identifier from two different places produces the same result in both: navigate to any of the seven primary views; retry the last run or the drafted run; open a named Settings tab, Hooks included; save settings; open a Library tab (artifacts, proposals); install an available update; restart the application; open the cloud account page; start project initialization; resolve project initialization; focus a named session; undo a proposal. No banner or empty-state action performs Git remediation. The terminal window's empty-state "Go to Workspace" action (REQ-SHELL-083) produces the same result as the corresponding view-navigation action offered from a banner.
  > **Narrowed by [`PD-SHELL-005`](../../product/directives/pd-shell-005.md#pd-shell-005) revision 2, 2026-10-22.** The list gains one action: cancel the running update download
  > (REQ-STARTUP-170). No other action is added, and every other clause still governs.
- **REQ-SHELL-116**: The shell's standard error surface is a persistent ERROR notice with title, message, optional copyable details, and an optional action; it is used for Git refresh failures, checkpoint failures, settings save failures, run failures, and similar.
- **REQ-SHELL-117**: Long-running worktree-merge and checkpoint operations show an indeterminate, window-modal, non-cancellable progress dialog that appears immediately, titled "Merging worktrees" / "Checkpoints" with accessible names "Worktree integration progress" / "Checkpoint progress".
- **REQ-SHELL-118**: The update check begins only after the window is visible. Exactly one waved-off version is remembered at a time, never a list, so a release newer than the waved-off one is offered afresh. An available update raises a persistent INFO notice titled "Rotaris {version} is available" carrying the action "Update now". Download progress is reported into that same notice in whole percentage steps, without a second notice appearing. While the download runs the notice keeps that title and its identity — it is updated in place, never replaced — and its message SHALL convey two things and no more: that the update is downloading, and how far it has got as a whole-number percentage, redrawn only when that whole number changes. The "Update now" action SHALL be disabled for the duration so the same download cannot be started twice, and its accessible description SHALL say why it is disabled. When the download completes the message SHALL convey that the update is ready to install and the action SHALL be enabled again, performing the install action of REQ-SHELL-115. When it fails the notice SHALL become the persistent ERROR notice of REQ-SHELL-116, whose message conveys that the download failed, whose copyable details carry the reason, and whose action is enabled again so the user can retry. The wording of all four messages is left to the implementation. Dismissing any banner other than the update notice never records a waved-off version.

  > download runs the notice keeps its identity and is updated in place, but carries REQ-STARTUP-118's title and message
  > and is not persistent; *revision 2:* its action cancels the download and is enabled throughout (REQ-STARTUP-170), and
  > a verified download leads not into the apply but into REQ-STARTUP-174's persistent ready notice with `Restart now`

  > installed` in place. The available-update notice and the waved-off rule are unchanged.

### Window close, quit protection, shutdown

- **REQ-SHELL-119**: Window-close guards are evaluated in this fixed order, and any of them can abort the close: (1) with unsaved settings present, a user-initiated close raises the save/discard/cancel dialog of REQ-SHELL-124, where Cancel aborts the close; (2) while a worktree integration is running, the close is refused unconditionally by an informational dialog titled "Integration in progress" whose body reads "Keep Rotaris open until the isolated worktree integration finishes."; (3) with runs active, a user-initiated close asks "Quit while a run is active?" — "Quit while {n} runs are active?" for more than one — states that Rotaris will request shutdown of the run(s) and that in-flight work may not finish, and offers Yes and Cancel with Cancel as the default; any answer other than Yes aborts the close.

  > window while a run is active?" ("…while {n} runs are active?") while that window's own project has a run active,
  > states that Rotaris will stop the run(s) and that in-flight work may not finish, and its Yes stops them before the
  > window closes; with no run active in its project it asks nothing.

- **REQ-SHELL-120**: Once the guards pass, the window is committed to closing: no new background work may start on the closing window.
- **REQ-SHELL-121**: Shutdown is clean and complete, and is checkable from outside the process. Active runs are asked to stop before the process ends, and no run keeps consuming a model provider afterwards. Once the last main window has gone, no persona window, artifact dialog or terminal window is left on screen — the one running instance may hold several main windows at once (REQ-STARTUP-161), and closing one takes only its own auxiliary windows with it (REQ-SHELL-123). A diagnostics run that was in progress leaves a complete, readable diagnostics directory rather than a truncated one. An update download that was in progress leaves behind no partially written and no locked file. The shutdown itself raises no error dialog and produces no crash report.
- **REQ-SHELL-169**: A launch of the application that arrives while this instance is shutting down SHALL be refused rather than admitted, and SHALL be honoured once the shutdown has finished. Shutting down means the interval that begins when the ordered shutdown effects REQ-MONITOR-131 fixes begin and ends when the process ends, the force-exit allowance that requirement carries included; this document holds no copy of that allowance. Throughout that interval the instance SHALL build no window for the launch, SHALL open no workspace for it, and SHALL start and write nothing on its behalf — in particular the window REQ-STARTUP-162 would otherwise have an instance with no window open build for a launch SHALL NOT be built while this interval is running. Admitting the launch would mean two instances of the application briefly existed, which REQ-STARTUP-161 exists to prevent, at the moment the product is least able to reason about its own state; refusing is also the shape REQ-STARTUP-161 already has, since a launch never starts a rival process.

  The instance SHALL answer the launch rather than ignore it, and its answer SHALL convey that the application is shutting down. Silence is not equivalent to refusal here: a launch that goes unanswered treats the instance as unreachable and reclaims the record after the interval REQ-STARTUP-164 fixes, which is the path that requirement keeps for a process that *cannot* answer, and taking it for a process that can answer would produce the two simultaneous processes this refusal exists to avoid.

  What the user is told. Where a main window of the shutting-down instance is on screen, that window SHALL be surfaced on the terms REQ-SHELL-161 fixes, and it is already stating that stopping is under way and how much of the allowance remains (REQ-MONITOR-283); no dialog, banner, notification or error SHALL be raised in addition, and no surface SHALL report that a launch was refused. Where no window of that instance is on screen, the user SHALL still be told that the application is finishing shutting down and will open when it has, so that a launch which appears to do nothing for the length of the allowance is not mistaken for one that failed. REQ-STARTUP-162's silence governs a launch that has been handed over and has ended; a launch that has been refused and is waiting is neither, and what that waiting launch shows is domain 11's to state rather than this document's.

  What happens once the shutdown has finished. The refused launch SHALL resume resolving the instance record on the terms REQ-STARTUP-164 fixes, and SHALL reach the outcome that requirement requires — exactly one instance running and a window on screen — later than an admitted launch would have reached it rather than not at all. Where the record was released as the process ended, the launch becomes the running instance and opens a window, honouring any workspace directory it carried exactly as REQ-STARTUP-163 requires of a launch that becomes the instance. Where the record instead passed to a successor rather than being released, as it does during the update restart of REQ-STARTUP-121, the launch is handed to that successor, which is not itself shutting down; in neither case does a third process start. The wait is bounded, because the force-exit allowance guarantees the process ends; and where a force exit leaves the instance record behind, REQ-MONITOR-131 having permitted the ordered effects to be left incomplete, the launch resolves that record exactly as REQ-STARTUP-164 already resolves one naming a process that is no longer running. A waiting launch SHALL NOT hold the exiting instance open, delay its teardown, or change when the force exit falls due: the refusal costs the user the remainder of the shutdown and nothing else.
- **REQ-SHELL-122**: The count the quit prompt reports is the number of runs active at that moment: on a host that coordinates several sessions it is the number of sessions currently running; on a single-run host it is 1 while a run is active and 0 otherwise. The prompt's singular and plural wording follows that number (REQ-SHELL-119, REQ-SHELL-044). A session counts as an active run exactly when its displayed status (REQ-SHELL-036) is busy under REQ-NOTIFY-050, which is the single definition of that test; this document holds no copy of the member list it draws on. Those are the statuses in which work is still in flight and a model provider may still be consumed, and the remaining members of REQ-NOTIFY-048 do not count, because quitting in those states loses no in-flight work, which is the only thing the prompt warns about. That same four-status test is what "a run is active" means throughout this domain: the workspace-open gate of REQ-SHELL-021, the close guard of REQ-SHELL-119, and the refusals of REQ-SHELL-125 and REQ-SHELL-126 all apply it. REQ-SHELL-155 is the one exception and states its own stricter test, under which a run whose liveness cannot be determined counts as live.
  > **Narrowed by [`PD-SHELL-009`](../../product/directives/pd-shell-009.md#pd-shell-009), 2026-10-07.** At a close that leaves another
  > main window open, and at REQ-SHELL-021's gate 3 (whose "a run is active" is this test), the count is the number of
  > runs active in the window's own workspace; at the last main window's close it is the instance's.
- **REQ-SHELL-123**: Auxiliary windows (persona, terminal) belong to their main window and are destroyed with it even if an explicit close was missed; a terminal window that outlives its stream attachment shows retained screens read-only.
- **REQ-SHELL-124**: The unsaved-settings dialog is a warning titled "Unsaved settings" asking "Save workspace settings before leaving?" with the informative line "Discard restores the last saved values." and buttons Save (default), Discard (destructive), Cancel. Save proceeds only if saving succeeds (a failed save blocks the transition); Discard restores the saved values and proceeds; Cancel blocks. The same gate protects leaving the Settings view, opening another workspace, and closing the window.
  > **Narrowed by [`PD-SHELL-010`](../../product/directives/pd-shell-010.md#pd-shell-010), 2026-10-14.** At a window's close (and at
  > REQ-SHELL-021's gate 1) the dialog is the renderer's: a renderer that cannot answer — gone, failing main's 5 s liveness
  > probe, or reported `unresponsive` — raises none and blocks nothing, and the close or the quit proceeds. Leaving the
  > Settings view is unchanged. Written under [OQ-297](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/60-spikes-and-later-questions-27.md#oq-297).

### Sessions: new, browse, focus

- **REQ-SHELL-125**: Starting a new session while a single-run host has an active run is refused with the error notification "Finish or cancel the active run before starting a new session."

  > refusal above no longer states what this product does: every window is the multi-session host,
  > so the dialog opens while a run is active with isolation locked on, and the previous run carries
  > on in the background. The text above is kept as the behavioural target it was derived from.
  Otherwise a modal dialog titled "Start session", at least 460 px wide, collects two optional choices. The first is Git worktree isolation, for which the user either supplies a branch name — accepted only when it is already in the normal form of REQ-GIT-009 and drawing that requirement's rejection error otherwise — or accepts the generated default `rotaris/session/<session-id>` (REQ-GIT-007), which the dialog shows before it is accepted and which receives the deterministic `-2`, `-3`, … collision suffixes of REQ-GIT-010 when the name is already taken. The second is the session's sandbox mode, whose vocabulary is exactly the three values of REQ-SANDBOX-048 — `off`, `workspace-write`, `read-only` — offered in that order and preselected to the effective configured value of `runtime.sandbox_mode`, so that accepting the dialog without touching the selector starts the session on the configured mode exactly as REQ-TOOLS-114 requires; the dialog offers no fourth value and no free-text entry, so an invalid mode is unreachable from this surface. Cancel ends the flow and clears nothing. Accepting clears session state, closes every pop-out window, dialog and terminal window, empties the composer, and leaves the Workspace view showing. On a multi-session host the previous session's focus is released first, and its run carries on in the background.

### Theming

- **REQ-SHELL-128**: Three built-in themes are offered in order: "rotaris-dim" (the default), "nocturne", and "high-contrast". Requesting an unknown theme name degrades silently to the default — a stale stored value must never prevent startup or raise an error.
- **REQ-SHELL-129**: Each theme declares a light/dark flag, token groups (color, spacing, radius, size, typography, motion, elevation), and hard accessibility floors of 4.5:1 for text contrast and 3.0:1 for boundary contrast. Default-theme shell metrics: navigation rail width 68, nav item width 54, status dot 6, focus ring 2, hairline 1, title bar height 44, status bar height 26 (px); spacing scale 4/8/12/16/24; corner radii 5/8/14 and a fully rounded "pill" value.
- **REQ-SHELL-130**: Applying a theme updates, in effect: all styled application widgets, native dialog/menu/caret/disabled-text colors (a dark theme must not leave light native surfaces), every live top-level window (windows that are mid-close are skipped safely, without crashing), and finally notifies self-painting widgets. Re-selecting the already-active theme still applies fully (startup idempotence).
- **REQ-SHELL-131**: A theme choice is committed to the preference store at the moment it is made, so a choice followed immediately by a crash or a power loss is still in force at the next launch. A stored theme name is not rejected when it is read back; an unrecognised name degrades per REQ-SHELL-128.
- **REQ-SHELL-132**: End-to-end theme-switch guarantees: after a switch, no widget in any of the seven primary views retains a color from the previous palette; unsent composer text, the active view, and every divider's sizes are preserved; the choice persists and is reapplied on next launch; a stored name for a removed theme degrades to the default at startup; each built-in theme renders all seven primary views without error.
- **REQ-SHELL-133**: The Settings interface tab carries a theme dropdown with accessible name "Theme" and a non-empty accessible description, listing every registered theme by its display label and opening with the running theme already selected. A hint label beside the dropdown shows the description of whichever theme is currently highlighted. Every registered theme SHALL declare, alongside the name of REQ-SHELL-128, a display label and a one-line description. The label SHALL be a human-readable rendering of that theme's own name, SHALL be distinct from every other registered theme's label, and SHALL stay stable for the life of the name so that a user's mapping from label to theme does not shift between releases; its exact spelling is left to the implementation. The description SHALL convey what visually distinguishes the theme and whom it suits. For the three built-ins the descriptions SHALL convey, respectively: that `rotaris-dim` is the default, a dark theme at moderate contrast intended for long working sessions; that `nocturne` is a darker dark theme with deeper backgrounds, for low ambient light; and that `high-contrast` raises contrast well beyond the floors of REQ-SHELL-129 and is the theme to choose when the other two are hard to read. Choosing a theme applies it at once, with no relaunch. Which theme is active is legible as text and not by colour alone.
- **REQ-SHELL-134**: Derived colors (persona shades, chips) that miss their contrast floor on their actual background are corrected by raising perceptual lightness only — hue and saturation preserved, adjustment only upward, capped near-white — never by darkening.

### Motion and reduced motion

- **REQ-SHELL-135**: Reduced-motion resolution precedence: (1) an explicitly stored in-app setting; (2) OS-level detection — on Windows, the "Turn off all window animations" accessibility setting; on macOS, the "Reduce motion" accessibility setting; on Linux, the reduced-motion or animations-enabled preference the desktop environment publishes through its standard settings interface; (3) otherwise motion is on. Step (2) is consulted only where the host actually publishes such a signal: a platform that publishes none, and a signal that cannot be read for any reason, both fall through to step (3), so motion-on is the deliberate answer whenever the operating system has not said otherwise. The value is read at each animation start rather than cached, so flipping the in-app setting, or flipping the OS setting while the application runs, takes effect without relaunch.
- **REQ-SHELL-136**: Under reduced motion: status-dot pulsing never starts and dots rest at full opacity; toggle knobs land instantly; spinners paint a static arc with no animation timer running; and the transient acknowledgement surface behaves as REQ-NOTIFY-022 states, which this document does not restate. Outcomes are always preserved; only travel is removed.
- **REQ-SHELL-137**: Spinners: sizes small/medium/large are 12/16/24 px with stroke widths 1.25/1.5/2.0; one revolution takes 800 ms at 16 ms frames with a 90° head arc over a faint track; the default accessible name is "Working" (an unnamed turning shape is invisible to a screen reader); animation runs only while the spinner is visible.

### About & Legal

- **REQ-SHELL-138**: The Settings view has an "About" tab that opens with no workspace or provider configured, and the command palette carries a shortcut-less "About & Legal" entry that opens Settings on that tab.
- **REQ-SHELL-139**: The About page renders exclusively from local data — zero network requests at render time.
- **REQ-SHELL-140**: The Product card shows rows Version (`v{version}`), Build (a build identifier, or an em dash when absent), Installation, Publisher, and Security contact. Values are text-selectable and announce "{name}: {value}". Fixed values: publisher "Concrete Dynamics UG (haftungsbeschränkt)", security contact "security@concrete-dynamics.com", product license "GPL-3.0-only". Installation reads "Standalone build" for frozen/standalone builds and "Package installation" otherwise; the build identifier is read from a bundled text asset and treated as absent when missing or unreadable.
- **REQ-SHELL-141**: The Legal documents card offers one link-style button per document, in order: Privacy Policy, EULA, Terms / AGB, Acceptable Use Policy, Withdrawal / Widerrufsbelehrung, resolving to the paths `/privacy`, `/eula`, `/terms`, `/acceptable-use`, `/widerruf` under the base `https://rotaris.ai`. Each button's text is the document name, its accessible name "Open {name}", its description "Opens {url} in the system browser". Activation opens the URL in the default browser; a failed open shows a small red caption "Could not open a browser for {name} ({url})." and the page stays usable; a subsequent success hides any prior error.
- **REQ-SHELL-142**: The Licensing card shows the selectable line "Rotaris is licensed under the GPL-3.0-only License." and a secondary button "Open third-party licenses" (description: opens the third-party license bundle shipped with this build). If the bundled notices file is missing or unreadable, a caption reads "This build does not carry a third-party license bundle."; otherwise the file opens as a local URL; a failed open shows "Could not open the third-party license bundle."
- **REQ-SHELL-143**: Every About-page control is keyboard-reachable and carries an accessible name.
