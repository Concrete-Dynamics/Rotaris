**OQ-530 — Do the configuration categories keep their deferred Save and Discard beside Interface's immediate controls, and how does a control then say which policy it follows?** *(REQ-CONFIG-270, REQ-CONFIG-286, REQ-CONFIG-399, REQ-SHELL-124, REQ-SHELL-041. Raised 2026-11-03 by [R-093](../../../product/requests.md#r-093), guide 03 §8 item 2 ([the guide](../../../research/design/03-information-architecture-within-a-fixed-structure.md)) of the design research corpus and backlog row 41 ([the backlog](../../../research/design/backlog.md)); numbered from OQ-493: OQ-462 to OQ-492 are the hardening orchestration's.)*

**What is open.** REQ-CONFIG-270 and REQ-CONFIG-286 hold configuration changes for Save and Discard behind REQ-SHELL-124's guard. REQ-CONFIG-399 makes the Interface category “**immediate** … not gated by Save or Discard”. REQ-CONFIG-286's guard says only that Discard does not undo theme and reduced motion. Guide 03 §8 D03-2 cites platform guidance that a changed setting applies at once, with no confirmation button. Nothing says how a control shows which policy it follows.

**What the build proceeds under.** Both policies as specified: REQ-CONFIG-270's and REQ-CONFIG-286's deferred Save and Discard, and REQ-CONFIG-399's immediate Interface category.

**What decides it.** One ruling: the deferred Save stays, and how a control signals its policy goes to the Settings brief, or a directive moves the configuration categories to immediate apply.

**Ruled 2026-11-03 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended keeping the deferred Save as a reading, with one rule for the Settings brief: each category has one commit policy, and a control whose policy differs from its category's says so in words in its own hint; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended the same reading, with the policy stated once per category and per control only where it departs, bounded by REQ-CONFIG-418. Ruled: the deferred Save stays, and the signal is both halves. The specification has already decided the split the entry reads as a gap: REQ-CONFIG-417 names five persistence destinations, REQ-CONFIG-418 confines Save and Discard to destination 1 and says the exclusion "is deliberate and is not narrowed", REQ-CONFIG-260 validates a save before any file is touched, REQ-CONFIG-283 and REQ-CONFIG-284 give a failed save a dirty form and a Retry, and REQ-CONFIG-288 refused, in a security-relevant control, to let one write commit half-finished edits — which is what applying every change of destination 1 at once would do to the six confinement and egress fields. The operator loses nothing the platform guidance [35] of D03-2 protects: REQ-CONFIG-262 starts a run from the values on screen, REQ-CONFIG-472 redraws the transcript the moment a tool-row control moves, and destinations 2 to 5 already apply on change; deferral delays the file, not the effect, so the guidance is weight for preferences, where the record follows it, and no ground for the configuration categories. How a control says which policy it follows is read from the record's own method, not invented: REQ-CONFIG-280 and REQ-CONFIG-434 convey state in words with colour only reinforcing, REQ-CONFIG-417 and REQ-CONFIG-471 make the diagnostics card say in words that it writes at once and is not undone by Discard, and REQ-CONFIG-418 requires the Discard explanation and the guard's text to say what Discard does not undo. The rule the Settings brief applies is therefore: each category states its commit policy once (the save bar and its indicator are that statement for a deferred category; Interface and Hooks state in words that their controls apply on change), and a control whose policy departs from its category's says so in words in its own hint — Runtime's released-run switch and answer-wait budget (REQ-CONFIG-408) and the diagnostics toggle, and in Display the verbosity slider and Show reasoning beside the four tool-row controls, whose hint says both that the transcript changes at once and that Save decides what the file says. Colour, an icon or position never carries the signal alone; wording stays the implementation's (REQ-CONFIG-286, REQ-SHELL-041). REQ-SHELL-041's flag and PD-WORKSPACE-007 are the owner's text and stand as written; their relation to REQ-CONFIG-262 is not decided here. No requirement is departed from and §1 is untouched: no directive. `design-ui` on Settings writes the brief's commit-policy statement and the list of departing controls, `build-ui` draws it, and `review-ui` checks that every departing control carries its hint and that the Discard explanation and the guard match REQ-CONFIG-418. Reopened by a measured finding on the built Settings view that an operator cannot tell which policy a control follows once the statements are drawn, by autopsy evidence of a Discard pressed to undo a control that applied at once, or by an owner's request to apply the configuration categories at once, which would be a directive against REQ-CONFIG-270, REQ-CONFIG-285, REQ-CONFIG-286, REQ-CONFIG-418 and REQ-SHELL-124.
