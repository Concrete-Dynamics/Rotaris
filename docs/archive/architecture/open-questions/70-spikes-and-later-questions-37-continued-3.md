**OQ-516 — Is a waiting approval seconds-critical, so the first opens at once and each next one as the dialog closes, or is each announced and opened by one command at the operator's breakpoint?** *(REQ-CHAT-103, REQ-TOOLS-063, PD-CHAT-002. Raised 2026-11-03 by [R-093](../../../product/requests.md#r-093), guide 06 §8 items 1 and 2 ([the guide](../../../research/design/06-ux-patterns-for-agent-products.md)) and guide 05 §8 item 16 ([the guide](../../../research/design/05-interaction-patterns-for-desktop-tools.md)) of the design research corpus and backlog rows 25 and 26 ([the backlog](../../../research/design/backlog.md)); numbered from OQ-493: OQ-462 to OQ-492 are the hardening orchestration's.)*

**What is open.** REQ-CHAT-103: “the first pending approval **auto-opens** its modal (questions wait for a click)”. PD-CHAT-002 narrows REQ-CHAT-103 for questions only. REQ-TOOLS-063: requests are “shown one at a time … the next queued request is presented as soon as the open dialog closes”, with a count of the others. Guide 06 §8 D06-1 cites interruption research: delivery at a breakpoint lowered frustration without slowing relevant answers. Guide 06 §8 D06-2 finds that when finishing one item reveals the next, people drain the queue and neglect their main task. Guide 05 §8 D05-16 finds grouped proposals accepted faster, though grouping hides a call that differs.

**What the build proceeds under.** REQ-CHAT-103's auto-open and REQ-TOOLS-063's one-at-a-time sequence.

**What decides it.** One ruling on cadence: it stands, or approvals are announced and opened by one command, with a break between requests or like calls grouped (directives over REQ-CHAT-103 and REQ-TOOLS-063).

**Ruled 2026-11-03 under the owner's delegation (R-072).** Advocate A (the user's lens) recommended that the cadence stand, the first approval opening at once and each next one as the dialog closes, with the queue question left to OQ-517 and a driven measurement named as what reopens it; Advocate B (the system's lens) recommended the same cadence with no directive, a one-command opener refused as a control the specification does not have, and an autopsy finding named as what reopens it. Ruled: the cadence stands as written and seconds are critical. A waiting approval does not simply wait: it runs against `runtime.approval_timeout_seconds`, 300 s by default (REQ-CONFIG-126, REQ-TOOLS-055), and under [`PD-TOOLS-005`](../../../product/directives/pd-tools-005.md#pd-tools-005) one expiry marks the user away, ends every wait still outstanding, shown or queued, under the headless policy, and resolves every later ask of that run without a prompt. A request held back until a breakpoint the application cannot detect is a request whose clock runs unseen, and one miss costs the run its human gate, which is D06-1's own condition for immediate handling. The harm breakpoint delivery guards against, a keystroke landing in the wrong place, is already answered inside the dialog: REQ-TOOLS-063 places initial focus on Deny, gives no default action, and reads Escape and window close as Deny, so a stray keystroke never approves and the recoverable outcome is a Deny the agent may re-plan around. The announcement the corpus asks for already exists: REQ-MONITOR-175 and REQ-NOTIFY-252 show every waiting run as awaiting input in every session list, focused or not, and the dialog states how many others wait. A separate command to open an approval is not minted: a command naming its own action is within the delegation, as OQ-505's region commands are, but this one adds the deliberate step D06-1's last clause says reverses deferral's gain, and under the 300 s bound and PD-TOOLS-005 a deferred open costs the run its human gate. A pause between requests would depart from REQ-TOOLS-184's 100 ms budget for presenting the next queued dialog, and a grouped dialog would replace REQ-TOOLS-063's fixed contents, one detail box and exactly three answers for one request, where D05-16 itself notes that grouping hides the call that differs. Nothing is displaced: REQ-CHAT-103's auto-open, REQ-TOOLS-063's one-at-a-time sequence with its count, [`PD-CHAT-002`](../../../product/directives/pd-chat-002.md#pd-chat-002) and PD-ORCH-005 clause 9 stand unchanged, and no directive is minted because no requirement is departed from. The build proceeds under the adopted text and nothing executes. Habituation across a long queue is OQ-517's, the filled control OQ-518's and the waiting row's reading OQ-520's; this ruling settles cadence only and leans on none of them. It reopens on a measurement: guide 06 §5 item 9's driven count, review-ui seeding one bad request in a queue of ten under `pnpm app:drive` with the scripted dialect and finding it denied less often deep in the queue than at its head, or a `run-autopsy` over a live run's permission trail finding approvals answered in under about a second across a queue or waits expiring while the operator was present; on a change to REQ-CONFIG-126's default or PD-TOOLS-005's away rule that makes a late answer cost less than the queue; on a breakpoint signal the record gains, such as the composer idle or a run settling; or on a request for a breakpoint command or a grouped dialog, which must say how it squares with the timeout.
