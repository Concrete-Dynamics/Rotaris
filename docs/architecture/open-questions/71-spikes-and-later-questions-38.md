**OQ-473 — Is 5 s the right bound on a utility process's first answer on a loaded host, now that a run host that misses it stays gone until a restart?** *(OQ-462, ADR-0002 consequence 1, REQ-STARTUP-156. Raised 2026-11-04 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091)'s hardening bundle B1, building OQ-462 (2); numbered from the hardening plan's range OQ-469 – OQ-480.)*

**What is open.** OQ-462 (2) applies the run host's 5 s `hello` bound to both children and ends a child that misses it; (4) never forks the run host again. Together, a run host that answers in six seconds on a busy machine leaves the person without runs for the whole session. That is not hypothetical on this host: on 2026-11-03, with the CPU at 100 % from another session's proofs, a driven launch's run host wrote its first line about 5.8 s after main claimed the instance and missed the bound ("no reply to hello within 5000 ms"), and loc1's reviewer met the same miss on 2026-10-21 at 22 to 100 % CPU ([the note](../../archive/defects/a-launch-whose-run-host-misses-the-hello-bound-ends-on-an-unhandled-main-rejection.md)). Before OQ-462 such a launch ended with no window at all; now it paints and says "The run host did not start". The specification states no bound on a child's start, and REQ-STARTUP-156 bounds the first paint, not the run host.

**What the build proceeds under.** The ruling as written: 5 s for both (`RUN_HOST_READY_BOUND_MS`, `WORKSPACE_SERVICE_READY_BOUND_MS` in `apps/desktop/src/main/child-supervision.ts`), the bound counted from the `hello` request main sends after `app.ready` and the provider service, not from the child's own start.

**What decides it.** A ruling on the bound — kept, lengthened, or counted from the child's `spawn` — or on one fresh fork after a missed ready only (never after a run host that ran, ADR-0002 consequence 1), which the specification does not address.

**OQ-475 — Does an error React recovered from on its own owe the person a notice, or only a line in the log?** *(REQ-NOTIFY-001, REQ-NOTIFY-003, REQ-NOTIFY-023, PD-MONITOR-002. Raised 2026-11-04 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091)'s hardening bundle B2, building [hardening §3.2 row 4](../hardening/02-the-findings.md#32-failure-handling-outside-main); numbered from the bundle's range OQ-475 – OQ-476.)*

**What is open.** Every window's root is now made with React's three error options, and each reports through `notice.report` (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/render-failure.tsx`). Two are failures the person sees — an error the window's boundary caught (its fallback is drawn) and one no boundary caught (the window's tree is removed) — and REQ-NOTIFY-003 makes a failure a persistent notice. The third, `onRecoverableError`, is React's report of an error it recovered from by drawing the tree again: the window looks as it did and nothing was lost. It is none of REQ-NOTIFY-003's three (a failure, a refusal, an outcome carrying a recovery action), so the specification asks no notice of it; but a renderer has no path to main's application log other than `notice.report`, and a recovered error that is only written to the window's console is the silence this bundle was built to end. The specification has no word on a renderer's own diagnostics.

**What the build proceeds under.** A persistent notice at `warning`, titled "This window recovered from a drawing error", its detail the error and both stacks, and so ONE `warning` line in main's log (`RENDER_RECOVERED_TITLE`, `renderFailureReport`); the two failures at `error`, as the hardening plan asks.

**What decides it.** A ruling on whether a recovered error is shown to the person at all — the notice at `warning`, at `info`, or none — and, if none, whether the renderer gains a log-only path to main (a command that writes one line and raises nothing), which would be a new member of the command union.

**OQ-476 — Is a main window whose renderer was `killed` owed a reload or a notice, now that every other loss is reloaded and told?** *(REQ-STARTUP-161, REQ-SHELL-020, REQ-NOTIFY-001, REQ-NOTIFY-003, ADR-0055, PD-MONITOR-002. Raised 2026-11-04 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091)'s hardening bundle B2, building [hardening §3.2 row 5](../hardening/02-the-findings.md#32-failure-handling-outside-main); numbered from the bundle's range OQ-475 – OQ-476.)*

**What is open.** `MainWindows` reloads a main window once within 60 s when its renderer is lost and raises a persistent error notice, for every reason Electron gives but `clean-exit` and `killed`, as the hardening plan asks (`RENDERER_ENDS_NOT_RELOADED`, `apps/desktop/src/main/main-windows.ts`). `killed` is what Chromium reports for a renderer ended from outside with an ordinary exit status: on Windows Node's `process.kill` and Task Manager's End task, both `TerminateProcess` with status 1 (measured on Windows on 2026-10-20 and again 2026-11-04 by `exit-trace-proof`), and, by Chromium's reading of a termination status and not measured here, a `SIGKILL` on Linux, the out-of-memory killer's among them. Such a window is left as it was before this bundle: open and blank, with main's `RENDERER GONE (killed, exit 1)` line at `error` and nothing said to the person. Whether a person ending the renderer means the window to stay blank, and whether the out-of-memory killer's end is a loss to recover from, the specification does not say.

**What the build proceeds under.** `killed` and `clean-exit` are neither reloaded nor told; every other reason is (`crashed`, `oom`, `abnormal-exit`, `launch-failed`, `integrity-failure`, `memory-eviction`).

**What decides it.** A ruling on `killed`: left as it is, told by a notice without a reload, or reloaded within the same bound as a crash.

**OQ-541 — Which window is PD-STARTUP-011's "window that holds no workspace", when every main window is built for a folder and its capture is fixed then?** *(PD-STARTUP-011, REQ-STARTUP-131, REQ-STARTUP-129, REQ-SHELL-163, REQ-SHELL-010. Raised 2026-11-04 by [R-091](../../product/requests.md#r-091)'s acceptance plan `hrda`, building [OQ-480](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/71-spikes-and-later-questions-38-continued-4.md#oq-480)'s ruling; numbered as the next free number on `origin/main` when it was filed.)*

**What is open.** [`PD-STARTUP-011`](../../product/directives/pd-startup-011.md#pd-startup-011) gives a window "that holds no workspace and whose `diagnostics/outputDir` is unset" the base `<per-user data directory>/diagnostics/rotaris`, "fixed when the window is built", and its reasons name the windows it means: "the welcome window and a refused or failed open, the launches where a fault leaves no other trace". In the build every main window is built for a folder — `MainWindows.build` is called with the launch's folder, the remembered one or the working directory (REQ-SHELL-010's resolution, `resolveStartup`), the chooser's, or a hand-over's — and the `(no workspace)` sentinel is never passed to a build (`setWorkspace` has no caller), while the capture starts when the window is built, before its workspace is opened (`DiagnosticsCaptures.open` in `apps/desktop/src/main/diagnostics.ts`, called from the adapter's `create` in `main/index.ts`). Measured on 2026-11-04 with `pnpm app:drive` at `light` on Windows 11 ARM64: a launch given no folder built its window for the working directory and wrote its run directory under that folder's `.rotaris/`; a launch given a regular file had its open refused (`workspace.not-a-folder`, "This workspace is a file, not a folder") and its capture inactive with `ENOTDIR` under `<file>/.rotaris/diagnostics/rotaris`, so the refused open the directive names keeps no log at all. No launch reaches the per-user base.

**What the build proceeds under.** As ruled: a window built with no workspace and no chosen folder takes the per-user base, fixed when it is built (`diagnosticsBase`, `perUserDiagnosticsBase`, `main/diagnostics.test.ts`), and the card shows it as the folder in force (`DiagnosticsCaptures.folderFor`); a window built for a folder keeps that folder's default whatever its open then does, and is inactive where that base cannot be created.

**What decides it.** A ruling on which windows take the per-user base: (a) a window whose own default base cannot be created when it is built (a file, an unwritable or unreachable folder) takes the per-user base instead, so the base stays fixed at build and the inactive case is the per-user base itself being unwritable; (b) the capture waits for the open to settle and takes the per-user base when it was refused or failed, which moves the window's start and gives up the lines before the open; or (c) as built, the per-user base only for a window built with no folder, which the product does not build today.

**OQ-542 — Does a first question saying that a damaged target's lock records could not be read meet REQ-SESSION-201's "stating how many there are"?** *(REQ-SESSION-201, REQ-SESSION-089, [OQ-477](../../archive/architecture/open-questions/69-spikes-and-later-questions-36-continued-4.md#oq-477), [ADR-0058 part 11](../adr/0058-the-workspace-database/11-corruption-domain-and-recovery-for-a-store.md). Raised 2026-11-04 by the hardening acceptance plan hrda's corrupt-store bundle, building OQ-477's ruling; raised as OQ-541, and renumbered OQ-542 when it landed, `main` having taken OQ-541 meanwhile.)*

**What is open.** REQ-SESSION-201's foreign-lock clause reads: "Where the target store holds such records, the restore MUST report them **together, as one condition covering all of them**, stating how many there are and what they mean". OQ-477 rules that no flow opens a target that failed C1's integrity check, and reads the clause as binding only where the product can establish that the target holds such records, which it cannot without reading them; so the restore's first question over such a target says that any lock records in it could not be read and states the clause's risk — a run on another machine reaching the store may be writing to it now, and the restore replaces what it writes — with proceeding never pre-selected (`unreadableTargetText`, `apps/desktop/src/main/store-resolution.ts`), and calls that the clause's purpose met with no words departed from. Building it found one case the reading does not reach: the clause's condition is the store holding such records, not the product knowing that it does, and a damaged store can hold them. `packages/workspace/src/workspace.test.ts`'s fixture for this ruling holds a lock row naming another machine on pages the restore never reads; the restore proceeds on the user's confirmation and no count was stated. The consortium's critic read the clause so when OQ-477 was ruled, and the ruling kept it as a reading. The specification does not say what a restore owes when such records may exist and cannot be read.

**What the build proceeds under.** OQ-477 as ruled, nothing minted: the first question states that the store failed its check with SQLite's report, that how many sessions it holds cannot be read and neither can any lock records in it, the risk the clause requires, and that every session in it will no longer be listed; Cancel is the default. The restore's own gates read no rows of such a target (`restoreStore`'s `targetUnreadable`).

**What decides it.** A ruling that the reading stands (the clause binds only where the records can be read, and is met), or that the words are departed from for a target that failed its integrity check — a directive narrowing REQ-SESSION-201's foreign-lock clause for that case, with what the confirmation states in place of the count.
